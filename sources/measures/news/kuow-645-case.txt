                                        The fate of the high-earners income tax, sometimes called the “millionaires tax,” is in the hands of Washington voters. This year, Democrats in Olympia passed a 9.9% tax on every dollar of household income over $1 million.

Almost immediately after the Legislature passed the measure, it was challenged, both in court and with an initiative on this November’s ballot.

The legal battle is ongoing, but voters will weigh in directly on the tax in November. A yes vote on Initiative 645, sponsored by Let’s Go Washington, would repeal the tax. A no vote would keep the tax.

Rian Watt, the executive director of the Economic Opportunity Institute, and former Congresswoman Jaime Herrera Beutler of the “yes” campaign answered questions about the tax as part of a debate moderated by KUOW’s Libby Denkmann. You can hear the full debate on KUOW’s “Soundside.”

On the financial implications of removing the tax:

Rian Watt: The case for a no vote on Initiative 645 is pretty simple. This initiative is an attempt by some of the richest people in our state, people making, by definition, over $83,000 a month, to cut $13 billion from K-12 education, from health care, and from higher education, and to do it all to give some of the richest folks in our state a massive tax break.

Jaime Herrera Beutler: Voting yes on Initiative 645 will not cut K-12 education funding, and it will not cut health care funding. This tax isn't even set to be collected until 2029. So only in Olympia can you cut something that doesn't even exist yet. And that money hasn't even been earned yet, so you're cutting something that isn't there. But beyond that, and I think more profoundly, is when lawmakers were offered a chance to earmark this money for health care, education, they said no multiple times.

On the potential for the income tax to reach lower tax brackets:

Rian Watt:  The governor has been really clear that he will veto any attempt to lower the threshold. Senior members of the Legislature have also been clear that they oppose any effort to lower the threshold. And they oppose it because, unlike this millionaires tax, an effort to do so would be deeply unpopular. Regardless of what those folks say and how voters interpret those statements, voters should understand that they are always going to have the final say on any attempt to lower the threshold.

Jaime Herrera Beutler:  I am way past the pinky promise thing with lawmakers. Two years ago, all these people that were just cited [state Sen. Jamie Pedersen] voted to never put in place an income tax. Now, two years later, they're like, "Well, it's only on rich people, so don't look behind the curtain here. It's only gonna be on these people, and we promise, we pinky promise, we double promise." Well, the lead sponsor of the bill who wrote this legislation, when he was asked about that flip-flop — the hypocrisy of that flip-flop — he quoted Mary Poppins, he said, "This is a pie crust promise, easily made, easily broken."

On the potential for the tax to lead to wealth flight and hurt the economy:

Rian Watt:  Everyone on the No on 645 campaign believes that Washington state should be a place where great businesses start, where great businesses grow, where they thrive. They're an important and critical part of our economy which is why, in part, this legislation put in place the largest tax break for small businesses in state history that entirely eliminated the business and occupation tax for a large range of businesses and then lowered the business and occupation tax for an additional set of businesses.

Jaime Herrera Beutler:  We already know that in 2025, 18,000 jobs were lost out of this area. We know that more are being discussed, 25% more of the small and medium-sized business. Everybody covers Starbucks and Amazon and all the big guys, who by the way, are not the ones who are gonna get targeted here. It's gonna be the small- and the medium-sized businesses who pay their taxes through their business.
