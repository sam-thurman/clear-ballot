Principles
Protecting Access to Justice
Every Washingtonian deserves a fair day in court — regardless of income, zip code, or background. Chief Justice Stephens has been a consistent voice for expanding access to justice across the state, earning the Access to Justice Board’s “Judicial Leadership” award alongside her colleagues. She will continue working to ensure our courts are open, efficient, and equitable for all.
Defending Judicial Independence
An independent judiciary is the cornerstone of democracy. Chief Justice Stephens has spent her career safeguarding that independence — training judges internationally on the rule of law, co-chairing the National and International Association of Women Judges’ Judicial Independence Committee, and serving as a Rodel Judicial Fellow focused on the courts’ role in strengthening democracy. She knows that when courts are free from political pressure, justice prevails.
Bringing Eastern Washington’s Voice to the Bench
As the first woman from Eastern Washington to serve on the state Supreme Court, Chief Justice Stephens understands the importance of a court that reflects the full geography and diversity of our state. She remains deeply connected to her Spokane roots and committed to decisions that work for communities on both sides of the Cascades.
Advancing Judicial and Civic Education
Chief Justice Stephens is a founding executive committee member of the National Courts and Sciences Institute and a Convener for the National Judicial College’s “Dividing the Waters” program, leading education programs on science and water law — issues of critical importance to Washington’s environment and economy. She understands that well-informed judges make better decisions for everyone.
In addition, Justice Stephens has a long history of civics education, working alongside K-12 educators to deepen understanding of our courts and the Rule of Law. She has long served with Washington’s Civic Learning Council and other civic education groups across the state to deepen understanding of our democratic values and promote civic engagement. .
Investing in the Next Generation
From teaching law students at Gonzaga to volunteering at youth justice events across the state, Chief Justice Stephens has always believed in building a stronger legal community for the future. As a young girl, she was inspired by the teachers and community members who saw something in her, and she continues mentoring the next generations of lawyers and advocates who will carry Washington’s justice system forward.
