Justice Theodore J. Angelis
Justice Theodore ("Theo") Angelis began serving on the Washington Supreme Court on April 4, 2026, after his appointment by Governor Bob Ferguson. Before joining the Supreme Court, Justice Angelis was an award-winning appellate and intellectual property litigator, an advocate who appeared in courts throughout Washington and the United States, a leader in the Bar and the community, and an author and editor of a leading treatise on Washington Law.
Justice Angelis was in private practice for 26 years, at K&L Gates LLP. He led the firm's Appellate practice and, more recently, served as global leader of its Intellectual Property practice. Justice Angelis represented some of the leading companies in Washington, but he also spent thousands of hours on pro bono cases. His philosophy is that all lawyers have a duty to protect those in need, expand access to justice, promote the rule of law, and use their skills to improve their communities. He has represented children who were denied counsel, veterans wrongfully detained, refugees, small businesses, and people experiencing homelessness. He also co-authored the White Paper that served as a founding document for the World Justice Project.
Although Justice Angelis' practice focused on appellate litigation and intellectual property, he also led appellate and trial teams in a wide range of cases: Washington and Federal constitutional claims; Internet-based crimes and misconduct; Tribal claims and disputes; claims against counties, cities, and public officials; and disputes involving development and real property. Justice Angelis is deeply immersed in Washington law, having served as the editor of the Washington Lawyers Practice Manual (WLPM) for more than a decade. Sales of the WLPM support the Neighborhood Clinics, which serve over 8,000 clients a year who otherwise could not afford legal representation.
Justice Angelis has been a leader in the Bar. He served as a Trustee for the King County Bar Association and led its Fair Campaign Practices Committee for nearly a decade. He was President of the M. Margaret McKeown Bar Association and the Middle Eastern Legal Association of Washington (MELAW), and he serves on the Foundation for Washington State Courts, which raises money to enhance security for Washington judges and courts.
Justice Angelis has received numerous recognitions and awards. He received the Founders Award in 2018 from Kids in Need of Defense (KIND) in recognition of his, and his firm's, broad commitment to assisting children in removal proceedings and his service on the KIND's Washington Advisory Council. His team received the ACLU's Humanitarian Award in 2017 in recognition of their class action litigation aimed at securing appointed counsel for children in immigration proceedings. He was named Citizen of the Year in 2016 by St. Demetrios Greek Orthodox Church in recognition of his service to the parish, its members, and the community. He was named Mentor of the Year in 2012 by the King County Bar Association in recognition of his efforts to build a broader and more diverse pipeline of future lawyers. In 2011, the Northwest Immigrant Rights Project awarded his team the Amicus Award for "groundbreaking work" while representing an army veteran wrongfully held. He also has been recognized as one of the leading lawyers in the United States by IAM 1000 , IP Stars , Chambers USA , Best Lawyers in America , and Washington Super Lawyers , and he was shortlisted for Washington Litigator of the Year in 2022.
Justice Angelis believes that it is critical that lawyers participate in improving the law and their communities. He co-chaired the Law Enforcement Committee of the Attorney General's Youth Internet Safety Task Force, which helped to change Washington law to facilitate prosecution of online crimes against children. He served on the Attorney General's Task Force seeking to protect unaccompanied minors in immigration proceedings. He was part of the team that successfully pushed to have Indian law included in the Washington State Bar Exam. He also has fought for religious freedom for Christians in Turkey (his mother's homeland) as an Archon of the Ecumenical Patriarchate. He is extremely active in the Greek Orthodox Church, having served as a Sunday School teacher, a member of the Parish Council and Diocesan Council, and as outside counsel.
Justice Angelis is a summa cum laude graduate of Claremont McKenna College, where he was student body president and won a British Marshall Scholarship. He has two Masters Degrees—from Oxford University and the London School of Economics. He graduated from Yale Law School, where he was Articles Editor of the Yale Law Journal and Managing Editor of the Yale Law & Policy Review . He also was named best oralist at the Thomas Tang National Moot Court Competition. Following law school, he was a law clerk to the Honorable Stephen F. Williams of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit.
Justice Angelis splits his time between Olympia and Seattle, and his first love is spending time with his wife and his two children.
