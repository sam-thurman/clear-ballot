ABOUT LISA
An engineer, a mom, and a budget writer for the 5th LD.

Lisa got into this work the way a lot of parents do. She watched what state budget decisions did to her kids' classrooms, and she decided the cleanest fix was to help write the budget herself.

Before Olympia, Lisa was a lead engineer and project manager at Boeing and a software consultant. She and her husband Bryan raised their son in the Issaquah Highlands, where she served two terms on the Issaquah School Board, including a stint as president.

In 2018, she became the first Democrat in modern memory to win the 5th LD's Position 2. She has won every election since by larger margins. In 2024, she took 56% of the vote.

In Olympia, Lisa serves as Vice Chair of the Capital Budget. In January 2026, she stepped in as Acting Chair and led negotiations on a $910 million supplemental budget that put more money into school construction, affordable housing, and flood response. She co-chairs the state's Children & Youth Behavioral Work Group and serves on the Education and Children, Youth, and Families committees.

The work has results. Five Callan bills passed the House in 2024 alone. Her HB 2180 raised the cap on special education funding and passed both chambers without a single no vote. Her early-learning bills helped lock in roughly $100 million for childcare construction. Her HB 1580 created the Children & Youth Multisystem Care Coordinator to find homes for kids stuck in hospital ERs.

THE RECORD
Where Lisa has put points on the board.

Three connected lanes: the Capital Budget, kids and behavioral health, and education. Here is what that has produced.

Schools and Special Education

Raised the cap on special education funding from 15% to 17.25% (HB 2180, passed without a no vote). Helped negotiate $1 billion for K-12 construction in the 2025-27 capital budget, plus $109 million more in the 2026 supplemental for small district and tribal compact schools and seismic upgrades.

Kids and Behavioral Health

Wrote HB 1580 to find homes for kids stuck in hospital ERs in psychiatric crisis. Co-chairs the state's Children & Youth Behavioral Work Group. Passed bills funding a behavioral health consult line for pediatricians and school counselors, and is moving HB 2038 to fund youth mental health through a surcharge on social media platforms.

Childcare and Early Learning

Locked in roughly $100 million for childcare construction through HB 2195 and HB 1314. Expanded early learning grants to tribal compact schools. Named 2024 House Champion of the Year by the Children's Campaign Fund.

Gun Safety

Voted for every major gun safety bill since 2019. Prime sponsor of permit-to-purchase, lost-or-stolen reporting, and bulk firearm purchase limits. Endorsed by the Alliance for Gun Responsibility.

Workers and Trades

96% lifetime score from the Washington State Labor Council. Voted yes on extending unemployment to striking workers. Supports apprenticeship at 15%+ on public works, prevailing wage, and Project Labor Agreements. Pushing apprenticeship into manufacturing and maintenance trades.

Climate and Clean Air

Voted for the Climate Commitment Act. Campaigned to defeat the I-2117 repeal effort. Capital budgets she has shaped include heat pump installations, weatherization, fish-passage culverts, and stormwater infrastructure.

Workforce Housing

Helped negotiate $776 million for housing and homelessness in the 2025-27 capital budget, plus $150 million more in the 2026 supplemental. Supports ADUs, lot splitting, and density along transit corridors. Focused on suburban East and South King County, where the price-to-income gap is widest.

Online Safety and AI

Prime sponsor of HB 2225, the Governor Request Bill regulating AI companion chatbots: detection of self-harm and suicidal ideation, content filters and disclosures for minors, and a ban on manipulative engagement techniques.
