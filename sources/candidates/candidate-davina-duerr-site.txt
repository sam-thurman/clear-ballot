ABOUT DAVINA
An architect who reads building permits and greenhouse-gas inventories with equal ease.

Davina Williams Duerr grew up in upstate New York, earned a Bachelor of Arts in architecture with a minor in environmental geography from Syracuse University, and moved west to build a career in design. A licensed architect, she brings a builder's eye for how housing, land use, and climate fit together to every bill she writes.

She stepped into civic life through the Bothell Landmarks Preservation Board — ten years of service, eight as chair. She co-founded the M.I.L.K. Money campaign through the Northshore Schools Foundation, a program that raises money for students experiencing homelessness and still serves thousands of kids each year.

In 2016 she ran for Bothell City Council and won. Her colleagues elected her deputy mayor. When state Rep. Derek Stanford was appointed to the Senate in 2019, the King and Snohomish County Councils appointed Davina to fill his seat. Voters elected her to a full term in 2020 and re-elected her in 2022 and 2024.

PRIORITIES
One problem, six domains.

Housing, climate, and transit are the same conversation. The rest — schools, community, local government — is the work that keeps the district livable.

HOUSING
3 bills signed
Make housing affordable.

Parking-minimum reform (HB 1183, first in the country). Middle housing. Streamlined permitting. Washington needs homes where jobs and schools already are — not one-acre subdivisions 30 miles down a freeway.

CLIMATE
4 bills signed
Reduce air pollution.

Buy Clean, Buy Fair (embodied carbon in public buildings). Industrial refrigerant phase-downs. Climate finally written into the Growth Management Act after two sessions of fighting for it.

TRANSIT
$532M for LD-1
Reduce trips on I-405.

Bus Rapid Transit stations. Sidewalks and protected bike routes. The 2025 transportation budget brought $464.5 million to the 1st District's stretch of 405 alone.

LOCAL GOVERNMENT
AWC City Champion
Answer the phone.

Chair of the House Local Government Committee. Point person for 281 cities and 39 counties. Special-purpose districts, annexation, public records — the work that doesn't make headlines.

SCHOOLS
Ongoing
Keep kids in school.

Co-founder of the M.I.L.K. Money campaign through the Northshore Schools Foundation. Raises money for students experiencing homelessness. Still runs today, still helps thousands of kids.

LEGISLATIVE RECORD
100+ bills sponsored.
Eight major wins.

The ones that matter. Parking, refrigerants, land-use, embodied carbon. The one that got away. Every one of them took two or three sessions to get right.

HB 2502
2026
Permitting reform to speed up housing

Davina’s 2026 permitting bill — streamlines local review and cuts delays so homes get built faster and at lower cost.

Signed into law
HB 1183
2025
Housing affordability and innovative housing products

First law of its kind in the country. Eliminates parking minimums for affordable, modular, mass timber, and passive buildings. Drafted with architects and builders.

Signed into law
HB 1462
2025
Cutting industrial refrigerants

Two years of work with NRDC and the refrigeration industry. Phases out super-pollutant refrigerants used in grocery coolers, heat pumps, and ACs.

Signed into law
HB 1154
2025
Landfill enforcement

Gives the Department of Ecology the power to step in on solid-waste facilities. One Washington landfill had been out of compliance for 20 years.

Signed into law
HB 2418
2025
Streamlined local permits for housing

Most consequential permitting reform. Allows cities to streamline review processes and reduce approval timelines for housing projects.

Signed into law
HB 1282
2022
Buy Clean, Buy Fair Washington Act

Takes on the embodied carbon in steel, concrete, and wood for state-owned public buildings. Among the first such laws in the country.

Signed into law
HB 1099
2022
Climate and the GMA (first attempt)

Passed the House. Died in the Senate at the end of session by a handful of votes. Came back in 2023 as HB 1181.
