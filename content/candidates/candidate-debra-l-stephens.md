## Background

Debra L. Stephens has been a member of the Washington State Supreme Court since January 2008 and began a second term as the court's chief justice in January 2025.[^courts-bio] She previously served as a judge on Division Three of the Court of Appeals.[^courts-bio] The court's biography says she was the first judge from that court and the first woman from Eastern Washington to join the Supreme Court.[^courts-bio] She is a native of Spokane and practiced law and taught as an adjunct professor at Gonzaga University School of Law before taking the bench.[^courts-bio] In the Voters' Pamphlet, she lists a law degree and a bachelor's degree from Gonzaga University and says she attended West Valley High School in Spokane.[^sos-pamphlet] The biography says she served for more than a decade as a school board director and chair.[^courts-bio] It also says she appeared as counsel more than 125 times before the Washington Supreme Court and as counsel of record in the U.S. Supreme Court.[^courts-bio] Under community service, she lists Upward Bound mentoring, the Minority and Justice Commission, YMCA Youth & Government, Habitat for Humanity, and past Rotary presidency, among others.[^sos-pamphlet] She and her husband have two grown children and a granddaughter.[^courts-bio]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Stephens emphasizes in her pamphlet statement and on her website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Rule of law and impartiality:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Stephens says she is "dedicated to upholding the rule of law and delivering fair and impartial justice."[^sos-pamphlet] She says decisions "must be based on the law and the facts."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Judicial independence:** She says that "in an era of growing attempts to politicize the judiciary," she works "to maintain the integrity and independence of our courts."[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says that "when courts are free from political pressure, justice prevails."[^site-principles]
- **Access to justice:** She says she has "a proven record of expanding access to justice, improving efficiency and transparency."[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says every Washingtonian "deserves a fair day in court — regardless of income, zip code, or background."[^site-principles]
- **Constitutional principles:** She says that as a former constitutional law professor she "respect[s] legal traditions and constitutional principles."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Eastern Washington and diverse perspectives:** She says that as the first woman from Eastern Washington on the court, she knows "that lived experience and diverse perspectives matter in making sound decisions."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Judicial and civic education:** Her website says she leads education programs on science and water law and has a long history of civics education with K-12 educators.[^site-principles]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Stephens lists roles including co-chair of the Board for Judicial Administration and of the National Association of Women Judges' Judicial Independence Committee.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists the Chief Justices Conference Executive Committee, the National Courts & Sciences Institute, the National Judicial College, and the Washington Civic Learning Council.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists community roles including former school board chair, Minority & Justice Commission, Sacred Heart Children's Hospital, past Rotary president, and the Washington Center for Performing Arts board.[^sos-pamphlet]

In the pamphlet, Stephens self-reports endorsements from her current and former colleagues, judges statewide, the Washington State Labor Council, elected officials, and education and community leaders.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website's self-reported endorsement list names Governor Bob Ferguson, former Governor Christine Gregoire, Lieutenant Governor Denny Heck, and Congresswoman Marilyn Strickland.[^site-endorsements] It also names Senate Majority Leader Jamie Pedersen, Speaker of the House Laurie Jinkins, and King County Executive Girmay Zahilay.[^site-endorsements] The site lists more elected officials, attorneys, and community members.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

Stephens has served on the Washington Supreme Court since January 2008 and is currently its chief justice, a role she also held in 2020 and 2021.[^courts-bio][^site-about] Her website says she was appointed to the court in 2008 and has since been re-elected three times.[^site-about] Before joining the Supreme Court she was a judge on Division Three of the Court of Appeals, which the pamphlet dates to 2007.[^courts-bio][^sos-pamphlet] The court's biography says she co-chairs the Washington Board for Judicial Administration, has worked with USAID to train foreign judges on judicial independence, and was named a Rodel Judicial Fellow in 2023.[^courts-bio] It lists awards including the Washington State Association for Justice's "Judge of the Year" and the Board for Judicial Administration's "Innovating Justice" award for leading the judicial branch during the COVID-19 crisis.[^courts-bio]

## Funding

As of October 15, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Stephens's campaign had raised $140,435.20 and spent $107,738.06.[^finance] About 5 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The top named contributors are Sean Gamble ($4,800, attorney, Friedman Rubin PLLP), Justice For All PAC ($4,800), and AFSCME Council 28 ($4,800).[^finance] They are followed by Mark Kamitomo ($3,600, attorney, The Luvera Law Firm) and John Allison ($2,500, attorney, Eymann Allison Jones PS).[^finance]
