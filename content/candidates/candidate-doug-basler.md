## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Doug Basler says he is the founder and president of Prologue Digital Inc., which he describes as a Washington advertising and video production company.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he has been a media and marketing professional since 1990 and a longtime sales trainer and small business owner.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he attended Multnomah University and lists continuing education through more than three decades of business, media, advertising, sales training, and leadership experience.[^sos-pamphlet] Basler says he has worked with rescue missions, the Union Gospel Mission, and the Salvation Army on homelessness outreach, and has worked with at-risk youth.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he is a former youth pastor and worship pastor and a longtime church volunteer.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website's About page repeats the same pamphlet biography.[^site-about]

## Positions

- **Economy and employers:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Basler says what he calls big government policies are making it harder to build, hire, afford, and stay in the Seattle area.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says people should be able to start a business, buy a home, learn a trade, and advance in a career.[^site]
- **Affordability:** Basler says families are squeezed by gas prices, energy costs, groceries, housing, insurance, and taxes.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Climate Commitment Act and gas tax:** Basler says the state's Climate Commitment Act is driving up energy costs while doing little to directly improve the environment.[^sos-pamphlet] He says that combined with the gas tax, these policies raise transportation, food, and energy costs.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Public safety:** Basler says he believes in safer communities.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says people in crisis should receive compassion while people are held accountable for crime and what it calls addiction-fueled disorder.[^site]
- **Families:** Basler says he believes in family, public safety, faith in God, and faith in each other.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he wants to support parents.[^site]
- **Approach to governing:** Basler says he will be a voice of common sense, moderation, and practical solutions and supports responsible government.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Basler's ballot listing says "Prefers Republican Party."[^kc-sample-ballot] In the Voters' Pamphlet, he lists service as a former King County Republican Party vice chairman, former president and current vice president of the Eastside Republican Club, and a longtime Republican precinct committee officer.[^sos-pamphlet] The Voters' Pamphlet statement does not list any endorsements.[^sos-pamphlet] The campaign website pages reviewed do not list endorsements.[^site]

## Record

Under "Elected Experience" in the Voters' Pamphlet, Basler lists Republican Party and Republican club positions, service as a precinct committee officer, and prior candidacy for U.S. Congress.[^sos-pamphlet] He does not list any public office.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

According to FEC filings, Basler's campaign raised $32,768.37 and spent $35,316.06 through September 30, 2026, and reported cash on hand of negative $2,004.72.[^finance] About 15.8 percent of receipts came from small donors, meaning unitemized individual contributions of $200 or less.[^finance] The largest contributor listed was Nirav Sheth ($5,000.00), with employer and occupation filed as retired.[^finance] Other top contributors were Don Kelts ($864.35, retired), the candidate himself, Douglas Michael Basler ($760.00, president of eztvspots.com), Jesse Ficks ($520.51, self-employed owner), and Paul Martin ($500.00, engineer at AT&T).[^finance]
