## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Sean O'Donnell says he has been a judge on King County Superior Court since 2013.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he has served as a Washington State Supreme Court justice pro tem, meaning a temporary fill-in justice for select cases.[^sos-pamphlet] He says judges statewide elected him president of the Superior Court Judges' Association.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he has served as chief of King County's family law and criminal departments, special inquiry judge, mental health court lead judge, and chair of a statewide court security task force.[^sos-pamphlet] Before becoming a judge, he says he was a prosecutor for 12 years and served on the Green River Task Force.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he tried Washington's first human trafficking case and was a special assistant United States attorney.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists a law degree, cum laude, from Seattle University School of Law and a bachelor's degree from Georgetown University, and says he is adjunct faculty at Seattle University School of Law.[^sos-pamphlet] Under community service, he lists the Rainier Scholars Resource Council and volunteer work as a mediator and mock trial judge.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website says he grew up in Bellevue and lives in Seattle with his wife and two children.[^site-about]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes O'Donnell emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Crime victims:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, O'Donnell says he has "centered crime victims' voices in our justice system."[^sos-pamphlet] His website says judges "can honor victims' rights while vigorously protecting defendants' constitutional guarantees."[^site-why]
- **Constitutional rights and impartiality:** He says that as a justice he "will defend our rights and the Constitution, maintain impartiality, and always remember that my rulings affect real people."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **How he decides cases:** His website says his framework begins "with the Constitution, the laws enacted by the Legislature, and the legal precedent that guides the Court."[^site-why] Precedent means earlier court decisions that guide later ones.[^site-why]
- **Protection orders and firearms:** He says that as chief family law judge he helped start a protection order court "that resulted in hundreds of firearms being surrendered by people who committed acts of domestic violence."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Court technology:** His website says he helped pioneer remote video jury selection during the pandemic and serves on the state bar's and the Supreme Court's artificial intelligence task forces.[^site-about]
- **Pretrial release and court security:** His website says he has worked to improve pretrial release decisions, including funding for a text reminder program to reduce warrants for missed court dates, and to improve courthouse security.[^site-why]
- **Access to justice:** His website lists "ensuring everyone has access to justice regardless of gender, race, disability, or language" among his values.[^site]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, O'Donnell lists the Rainier Scholars Resource Council and says he was rated "Exceptionally Well Qualified" by multiple bar associations.[^sos-pamphlet] His website names the King County Bar Association, the Washington State Veterans Bar Association, and the Cardozo Society of Washington State as the groups giving that rating.[^site-endorsements] His website says he is board chair for Lakeside School in Seattle and a past president of the Superior Court Judges' Association.[^site-about]

In the pamphlet, O'Donnell self-reports endorsements from Governor Christine Gregoire, Congressman Adam Smith, Congresswoman Marilyn Strickland, Lands Commissioner Dave Upthegrove, and Seattle City Council President Joy Hollingsworth.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists 15 mayors, county executives, the Washington Farm Bureau, the Washington Council of Police and Sheriffs, and more than 200 trial and appellate judicial officers.[^sos-pamphlet] His website's self-reported partial list adds former Governor Gary Locke, Justice Helen Whitener, former Justice Bobbe Bridge, Seattle Mayor Bruce Harrell, and the Washington Retail Association.[^site] The site lists more judicial officers, elected officials, victims' advocates, attorneys, and community members.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

The pamphlet lists O'Donnell as a King County Superior Court judge since 2013; it does not have a separate "Elected Experience" entry for him.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was elected to the King County Superior Court bench in 2013.[^site-about] It says he currently serves as lead judge of the King County Involuntary Treatment Act Court and as the county's special inquiry judge.[^site-about]

## Funding

As of October 15, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show O'Donnell's campaign had raised $445,711.74 and spent $89,877.68.[^finance] About 2.9 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The filings show $11,922.77 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors are Constantinos Christofilis ($5,000.31, finance director, Archon Capital Management LLC), Kristin Ferrera ($4,800, superior court judge, Chelan County), Matt Champagne ($4,800, chief executive officer, ScreenPal), and Build East PAC ($4,800).[^finance]
