## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, David Stevens lists his legal experience as Superior Court judge, senior felony prosecutor, federal public defender, and chief public defender for the Colville Confederated Tribes.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists work as a federal prosecutor, an international prosecutor in Pristina, Kosovo, and a justice advisor in Bamiyan, Afghanistan.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website says he was elected to Mason County Superior Court in November 2023 and re-elected in 2024.[^site-about] He says he joined the U.S. Navy after high school and later used veterans' benefits to attend the University of Washington.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he then worked as a federal civil rights investigator for the Fair Housing Enforcement Center in Seattle before attending the University of Washington School of Law.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists a law degree and a bachelor's degree from the University of Washington and a Department of State certificate in Islamic jurisprudence.[^sos-pamphlet] Under community service, he lists Mount Olive Lutheran Church, VFW Larry Godwin Post 1694 Honor Guard, Skookum Rotary, and mock trial judging for the YMCA and UW Law School.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he and his wife live in Shelton.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Stevens emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Role of the court:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Stevens says the court's duty is to uphold the constitution, and that "public policy is the province of the political branches and not the courts."[^sos-pamphlet] His website says the Supreme Court "is a constitutional court, not a policy-making body."[^site]
- **How to read the constitution:** His website says he believes the state constitution "should be interpreted based on its text and original meaning and not on evolving societal standards."[^site] It says changes to the constitution "must be done by constitutional amendment and not through judicial re-interpretation."[^site]
- **Elected versus appointed judges:** He says the state constitution "mandated the election of judges because direct appointment leads to ideological and political decisions," and that he was elected, not appointed.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Jury selection:** His website says the current Supreme Court "believes that judges should evaluate the selection of jurors based on Race," and that he believes "that is wrong."[^site]
- **Warrants:** His website says the current court "has limited the ability of trial judges to issue warrants when criminal defendants do not show up to court."[^site] He says courtroom experience matters "when liberty and public safety are at stake."[^site]
- **Trial experience:** He says he has tried more than 200 jury trials as a lawyer and judge.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says this gives "a practical understanding of evidence, procedure, and jury decision-making."[^site]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Stevens lists Mount Olive Lutheran Church, where he is a deacon, VFW Post 1694, Skookum Rotary, and the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys, where he has presented legal education seminars.[^sos-pamphlet] His website also lists the 40et8 Voiture 135 veterans' club and the Yesteryear Car Club.[^site-about]

In the pamphlet, Stevens says his supporters include tribes, judges, public defenders, prosecutors, attorneys, and law enforcement, without naming them.[^sos-pamphlet] His website's self-reported endorsement list includes the Washington Council of Police and Sheriffs, Washington Farm Bureau PAC, Tacoma Business Council, and Associated Builders and Contractors Inland Pacific.[^site-endorsements] It also lists the Washington Affordable Housing Council, Valley Landlords Associations, and the Hazara Community of Washington.[^site-endorsements] It also lists retired Supreme Court Justice Richard Sanders, four other judges, U.S. Representative Mike Baumgartner, and state Representatives Rob Chase and Jim Walsh.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

The pamphlet lists Superior Court judge as Stevens's current judicial role.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was elected to Mason County Superior Court in November 2023, defeating an appointed incumbent, and re-elected in 2024.[^site-about] The pamphlet does not have a separate "Elected Experience" entry for him.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

As of October 15, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Stevens's campaign had raised $79,125.50 and spent $64,891.76.[^finance] About 11.8 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The filings show $7,432.00 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors are the Council of Police Political Support ($4,800), George Petrie ($4,000, real estate, Goodman Real Estate), Goodman Real Estate Inc ($2,400), and Pedro Domingos ($2,400, retired).[^finance]
