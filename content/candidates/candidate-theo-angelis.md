## Background

Theodore ("Theo") Angelis began serving on the Washington Supreme Court on April 4, 2026, after Governor Bob Ferguson appointed him.[^courts-bio] Before joining the court, he was in private practice for 26 years at the law firm K&L Gates LLP.[^courts-bio] He led the firm's appellate practice and later served as global leader of its intellectual property practice.[^courts-bio] He graduated summa cum laude from Claremont McKenna College, holds master's degrees from Oxford University and the London School of Economics, and graduated from Yale Law School.[^courts-bio] After law school he was a law clerk, a lawyer who assists a judge, for Judge Stephen F. Williams of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit.[^courts-bio] The court's biography says he served as a trustee of the King County Bar Association and as president of the Middle Eastern Legal Association of Washington.[^courts-bio] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Angelis says he handled pro bono cases, meaning free legal work, for immigrant children, veterans, refugees, small businesses, and people experiencing homelessness.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he has Turkish and Greek ancestry and is the first justice of Middle Eastern descent on the court.[^sos-pamphlet] The court's biography says he splits his time between Olympia and Seattle and has a wife and two children.[^courts-bio]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Angelis emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Rule of law and independent courts:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Angelis says he has "fought for the rule of law, the rights of all individuals, and to ensure our courts remain fair, independent, and accessible."[^sos-pamphlet] His website says courts "must remain independent and impartial."[^site]
- **Commitment to the constitution:** His website says he believes the court's "foremost responsibility is to faithfully interpret and apply the Constitution and laws of Washington State."[^site]
- **Access to justice:** He says that before joining the court he "frequently volunteered to help people who couldn't afford lawyers."[^sos-pamphlet] His website says courts "must remain open, accessible, and fair to all people."[^site]
- **Immigration cases:** He says he sued the federal government when it required children as young as 4 to appear in immigration court without lawyers, and that the government "eventually agreed to pay for lawyers."[^sos-pamphlet] He also says he represented an Army veteran and naturalized citizen whom ICE "wrongly detained" for seven months.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Diversity in the legal profession:** He says he has worked to mentor attorneys "to expand diversity in our profession."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Consistent application of law:** His website says the Supreme Court "plays a critical role in ensuring that Washington's laws are applied consistently and fairly."[^site]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Angelis lists service as a trustee of the King County Bar Association and president of the Middle Eastern Legal Association of Washington.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists membership on the Attorney General's Youth Internet Safety Task Force.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists the Founders Award from Kids in Need of Defense, the ACLU Humanitarian Award, and the King County Bar Association's Mentor of the Year award.[^sos-pamphlet] The court's biography says he also serves on the Foundation for Washington State Courts.[^courts-bio]

In the pamphlet, Angelis self-reports endorsements from all of his colleagues on the Supreme Court, Governors Ferguson and Gregoire, former U.S. Attorneys Durkan, Gorman, and McKay, and King County Executive Zahilay.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists more than 500 judges and attorneys, 52 state legislators, mayors statewide, the Washington State Labor Council, and King County and numerous legislative district Democratic organizations.[^sos-pamphlet] His website's self-reported endorsement list names Chief Justice Debra Stephens, former Governor Jay Inslee, Congresswoman Marilyn Strickland, Lieutenant Governor Denny Heck, and Aberdeen Indivisible, among many judges and elected officials.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

Angelis has served on the Washington Supreme Court since April 4, 2026, when he joined the court by appointment from Governor Ferguson.[^courts-bio] Position 5 is on the ballot for a two-year unexpired term, meaning the remainder of the term of the justice he replaced.[^sos-pamphlet] Before his appointment, he spent 26 years as a lawyer at K&L Gates LLP, where he led appellate and intellectual property teams.[^courts-bio] The court's biography says he served as editor of the Washington Lawyers Practice Manual for more than a decade.[^courts-bio] It also says he co-chaired the Law Enforcement Committee of the Attorney General's Youth Internet Safety Task Force and served on the Attorney General's task force on unaccompanied minors in immigration proceedings.[^courts-bio] The biography lists awards including the Kids in Need of Defense Founders Award in 2018 and the ACLU's Humanitarian Award in 2017.[^courts-bio] It says the ACLU award recognized his team's class action litigation seeking appointed counsel for children in immigration proceedings.[^courts-bio]

## Funding

As of October 17, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Angelis's campaign had raised $463,356.01 and spent $455,042.53.[^finance] About 4.4 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The filings show $17,676.00 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors, each giving $4,800, are Richard Adler (attorney, Adler Giersch), Wendy Cochran (retired), Darrell Cochran (attorney, PCVA), and John Connelly (attorney, Connelly Law Offices PLLC).[^finance]
