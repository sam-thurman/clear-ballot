## Background

Davina Duerr is the state representative for Legislative District 1, Position 1, and is listed as a Democrat on her official legislature page.[^leg-member] She chairs the House Local Government Committee and serves on the Environment & Energy and Transportation committees.[^leg-member] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Duerr says she was appointed to represent the 1st District in 2019.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she served on the Bothell City Council from 2016 to 2022, including as deputy mayor from 2016 to 2020.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she is a licensed architect with a Bachelor of Architecture from Syracuse University and a minor in environmental geography.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website says she grew up in upstate New York and lives in Bothell.[^site] In the pamphlet, she lists service on the Eastside Transportation Partnership, the WSDOT Executive Advisory Group, the Sound Transit Bus Rapid Transit Elected Leadership Group, and the PSRC Transportation Policy Board.[^sos-pamphlet] She also says she spent ten years on the Bothell Landmarks Preservation Board, eight as chair, and co-founded the Northshore Schools Foundation MILK Money campaign for students experiencing homelessness.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

- **Overall priorities:** In the pamphlet, Duerr says quality of life depends on affordable homes near jobs, accessible transportation, a healthy environment, and strong public education.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Housing:** In the pamphlet, Duerr says her bills last term made it cheaper to build affordable housing and streamlined permits for new housing.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says Washington needs homes where jobs and schools already are, and lists parking-minimum reform, middle housing, and streamlined permitting as her work.[^site]
- **Climate:** Her website says she wants to reduce air pollution and points to refrigerant phase-downs, embodied carbon rules for public buildings, and adding climate to the Growth Management Act.[^site] Her website describes embodied carbon as the carbon in building materials such as steel, concrete, and wood.[^site] In the pamphlet, she says one of her bills phased out "super-pollutant refrigerants".[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Transportation:** In the pamphlet, Duerr says she secured $532 million for the 1st District in 2025, including $464.5 million for the I-405 corridor project.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she wants to reduce trips on I-405 through bus rapid transit stations, sidewalks, and protected bike routes.[^site]
- **Local government:** Her website says that as committee chair she is the point person for 281 cities and 39 counties on issues such as special-purpose districts, annexation, and public records.[^site]
- **Schools:** Her website says she wants to keep kids in school and points to the MILK Money campaign, which it says raises money for students experiencing homelessness.[^site]
- **Community representation:** Her website says she is a member of the Members of Color Caucus and pushed for bias-crime data collection and hate-crime response funding after the 2021 Atlanta shootings.[^site]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Duerr's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Democratic Party".[^kc-sample-ballot] In the pamphlet, she lists self-reported endorsements from the State Labor Council, Washington Conservation Action, Sen. Stanford, Rep. Kloba, and Mayors Thompson, Arndt, Herbig, and Curtis.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website lists self-reported endorsements from organizations including Washington Conservation Voters, Sierra Club, Washington State Labor Council, SEIU, First District Democrats, WSNA PAC, Machinists Local 751, and Moms Demand Action.[^site] The website lists more organizations, as well as U.S. Rep. Suzan DelBene, state legislators, county officials, and city officials from Bothell, Woodinville, Kenmore, Kirkland, and Lake Forest Park.[^site] Her website also lists awards it says she received, including Legislator of the Year from Washington Conservation Voters in 2022 and a City Champion Award from the Association of Washington Cities in 2021.[^site]

## Record

Duerr was the prime sponsor of 20 bills and resolutions in the 2025-26 term, according to the legislature's bills-by-sponsor report.[^leg-sponsored] Seven became law, including 2SHB 1183 on building codes, 2SHB 1154 on solid waste handling, 2SHB 1462 on hydrofluorocarbons, and E2SHB 2418 on permit review.[^leg-sponsored] In the pamphlet, Duerr describes the refrigerants covered by her bill as "super-pollutant refrigerants".[^sos-pamphlet] On the shortlist bills, Duerr voted yea on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parental-rights law created by Initiative 2081.[^leg-member] She voted yea on ESSB 6346 (2026), the bill creating the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million.[^leg-member] She voted yea on ESSB 5167, the 2025-27 operating budget, and on ESSB 5998, the 2026 supplemental operating budget.[^leg-member] She voted yea on the 2025 revenue bills ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax changes) and ESSB 5814 (sales tax on certain services).[^leg-member] She also voted yea on ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax surcharges), ESSB 5794 (repeal of certain tax preferences), and SHB 2077 (tax on certain zero-emission vehicle credit sales).[^leg-member] HB 1699, SB 5097, and SB 5012, the 2025 bills on girls' interscholastic sports, died in committee without a floor vote, so there is no vote to report.[^leg-member]

## Funding

According to PDC filings, Duerr's campaign had raised $70,170.23 and spent $44,682.86 as of October 13, 2026.[^finance] About 3.1% of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums campaigns report as small contributions.[^finance] The largest line item in the filings is $31,276.09 listed as "Miscellaneous Receipts", with no employer or occupation filed.[^finance] The next largest contributors, at $2,400 each, are the WA Affordable Housing Council, Puget Sound Energy, the WA Beverage Assoc PAC, and Elizabeth Nelson, an accountant at Nelson CPA.[^finance]
