## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Nirav Sheth says he served in the U.S. Marines from 2011 to 2014 and received an honorable discharge.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he was a police officer with the Lakewood Police Department in 2017 and resigned because of partial hearing loss.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website says he served in the Marines as an aircraft mechanic and military police officer in Yuma, Arizona, and worked as a Lakewood officer from May to October 2017.[^site-about] Sheth says he has been chief financial officer of Sheth Groups, which he describes as Meineke franchises, since 2017, and was co-owner of Bistro Baffi restaurant from 2018 to 2025.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists an associate degree in electrical engineering and computer science, plus training as an airframe mechanic and in business administration and human resource management.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was born and raised in Ahmedabad, India, came to the United States in 2008 as a student, and now lives in Pierce County.[^site-about] In the pamphlet, he lists volunteer service as an intelligence liaison with the U.S. Border Patrol in Yuma during his Marine service and as an American Red Cross volunteer.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

- **Housing and homelessness:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Sheth says his goal is to create sustainable and affordable housing to solve homelessness.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Public safety:** Sheth says he wants to bring what he calls law and order back to Seattle.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he supports funding to hire more police, keeping county sheriffs elected, and mandatory treatment for repeat drug-possession offenders.[^site-safety]
- **Cost of living:** Sheth says he wants a cheaper cost of living for everyone.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says state taxes, including the gas tax, the Climate Commitment Act, and the sales tax on services, are driving up costs.[^site-economy]
- **Energy:** Sheth says he wants to work on energy independence.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says the energy grid needs modernizing for national security and economic progress.[^site-infrastructure]
- **Federal spending:** Sheth says he will treat tax dollars with what he calls the same respect he applied to budgets in the Marines and in business.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Immigration:** His website says he supports securing the border while recognizing the role of immigrants in the workforce, and streamlining the immigration process for people who want to work.[^site-immigration]
- **Small business:** His website says he wants to reduce government regulations on small businesses and streamline bureaucratic processes.[^site-business]
- **Infrastructure:** His website says he supports maintaining and improving bridges and roads.[^site-infrastructure]
- **Education:** His website says K-12 education should receive 50 percent of the state budget and that curriculum should refocus on reading, writing, math, civics, and early literacy.[^site-education] It also says he supports a parental-notification initiative and a girls' sports initiative.[^site-education]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Sheth's ballot listing says "Prefers Republican Party."[^kc-sample-ballot] In the Voters' Pamphlet, he describes himself as a Marine veteran and former police officer.[^sos-pamphlet] The Voters' Pamphlet statement does not list any endorsements.[^sos-pamphlet] The campaign website pages reviewed do not list endorsements.[^site]

## Record

Under "Elected Experience" in the Voters' Pamphlet, Sheth writes that he has held no previous elected position.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

According to FEC filings, Sheth's campaign raised $11,810.81 and spent $10,525.00 through September 30, 2026, and had $5,043.77 in cash on hand.[^finance] About 5.6 percent of receipts came from small donors, meaning unitemized individual contributions of $200 or less.[^finance] The largest contributor listed was the candidate himself, Nirav Sheth, at $5,900.00, filed as a business owner at Sheth Group LLC.[^finance] The next largest were Doug Basler ($5,000.00, business owner at Prologue Digital) and Virginia Mason ($250.00, retired).[^finance]
