## Background

Suzan DelBene has served in the U.S. House for Washington's 1st Congressional District since 2012, according to Congress.gov, which lists her as a Democrat.[^congress-member] In the Voters' Pamphlet, DelBene says she serves on the House Ways and Means Committee.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she had a 20-year career as a businesswoman and entrepreneur, including as a Microsoft executive and leader of local high-tech startups.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website says she worked at Microsoft for more than 12 years and helped start drugstore.com and the startup Nimble Technology.[^site-about] DelBene says she was Director of the Washington State Department of Revenue, a role her website says Governor Chris Gregoire appointed her to in 2010.[^sos-pamphlet][^site-about] She lists a bachelor's degree in biology from Reed College and a master's degree in business administration from the University of Washington.[^sos-pamphlet] In the pamphlet, she says she has mentored students at the UW Business School, served on her church board, and volunteered with the PTA, Girl Scouts, and YWCA.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she chairs the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee and chaired the New Democrat Coalition in the 117th Congress.[^site-about]

## Positions

- **Cost of living and taxes:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, DelBene says she supports policies to lower the cost of living, reduce taxes for working families, and ensure the wealthy pay their fair share.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Child Tax Credit:** DelBene says she has led on permanently expanding the Child Tax Credit, which she says would send monthly checks to families and cut child poverty in half.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Health care:** DelBene says she is working to lower health care premiums and roll back cuts to Medicaid.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she has voted to protect the Affordable Care Act and supports giving people a choice between public and private coverage.[^site-issues]
- **Housing:** DelBene says she introduced bipartisan legislation to spur the creation of two million affordable homes.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Tariffs:** DelBene says she wants to end President Trump's tariffs, which she describes as reckless.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Immigration:** DelBene says it is past time to reform the immigration system and rein in ICE agents.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports comprehensive immigration reform and legal guardrails on ICE.[^site-issues]
- **Social Security and Medicare:** DelBene says she is committed to strengthening Social Security and Medicare.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Reproductive rights:** DelBene says she supports reproductive rights and will fight attempts to pass a nationwide abortion ban.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports the Women's Health Protection Act, which she describes as a bill to guarantee access to abortion care in every state.[^site-issues]
- **Climate and environment:** Her website says the Pacific Northwest is already experiencing climate change impacts and calls for action to combat climate change.[^site-issues]
- **Public safety:** Her website says she supports investing in community policing and crime diversion programs and addressing gun violence.[^site-issues]

## Affiliations and endorsements

DelBene's ballot listing says "Prefers Democratic Party."[^kc-sample-ballot] Her campaign website says she chairs the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee.[^site-about] In the Voters' Pamphlet, she says she has endorsements from Democratic groups, labor unions, and local leaders.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website lists self-reported endorsements including the Washington State Labor Council (AFL-CIO), King County Democrats, Senator Patty Murray, and Governor Bob Ferguson.[^site-endorsements] Other self-reported endorsements on the site include SEIU, the National Education Association, the Machinists District Lodge 751, the Alliance for Gun Responsibility, the Sierra Club, Planned Parenthood Action Fund, and the Human Rights Campaign.[^site-endorsements] The site lists many more elected officials, Democratic organizations, unions, and civic groups.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

Congress.gov lists 17 measures DelBene introduced in the 119th Congress as of October 17, 2026, including 15 bills and two House resolutions.[^congress-sponsored] Examples include H.R. 407, the Prevent Tariff Abuse Act; H.R. 8261, the Chronic Care Management Improvement Act of 2026; and H.R. 2250, the National Landslide Preparedness Act Reauthorization Act of 2025.[^congress-sponsored] H.R. 2250 was reported by the Committee on Natural Resources in January 2026; the other measures were referred to committee.[^congress-sponsored]

On the shortlist of appropriations and budget-reconciliation measures, DelBene voted nay on H.R. 1968, the Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2025.[^congress-member] A continuing appropriations act is a bill that keeps federal agencies funded for a set period, usually at existing levels.[^congress-member] She voted nay on H.R. 1, the 2025 budget reconciliation act; reconciliation is a fast-track process for bills that change federal spending or taxes.[^congress-member] She voted nay on H.R. 5371, the Continuing Appropriations, Agriculture, Legislative Branch, Military Construction and Veterans Affairs, and Extensions Act, 2026.[^congress-member] She voted yea on H.R. 6938, the Commerce, Justice, Science; Energy and Water; and Interior and Environment Appropriations Act, 2026.[^congress-member] She voted nay on H.R. 7147, the Homeland Security and Further Additional Continuing Appropriations Act, 2026.[^congress-member] She voted nay on H.R. 7148, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026.[^congress-member] She voted nay on S. 2, the Secure America Act, a reconciliation bill.[^congress-member] She voted yea on H.R. 6500, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027.[^congress-member]

## Funding

According to FEC filings, DelBene's campaign raised $4,130,100.81 and spent $4,230,550.68 through September 30, 2026, and had $963,270.74 in cash on hand.[^finance] About 22.1 percent of receipts came from small donors, meaning unitemized individual contributions of $200 or less.[^finance] The largest contributor listed was the DelBene Democratic Majority Fund ($283,617.00), a joint fundraising committee.[^finance] Four PACs gave $10,000 each.[^finance] They were the Laborers' International Union of North America PAC, the New York Life Insurance Company PAC, the National Cotton Council of America PAC, and the American Crystal Sugar Company PAC.[^finance] No employer or occupation was filed for these contributors.[^finance]
