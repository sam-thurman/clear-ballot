## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Dave Larson says he has 41 years of legal experience, including 18 years as a trial judge, now retired, and 23 years as a trial lawyer.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website says he served as a Federal Way Municipal Court judge from 2008 to 2025 and as its presiding judge from 2009 to 2025.[^site-record] He says he worked as a mediator and arbitrator from 1990 to 2008 and served as president of the Federal Way School Board.[^sos-pamphlet] A mediator helps parties settle a dispute; an arbitrator decides a dispute outside of court.[^sos-pamphlet] His website lists earlier work as staff trial counsel for Safeco Insurance, owner of his own law firm, and of counsel at the firm Williams Kastner.[^site-record] He lists a law degree from Seattle University School of Law and a bachelor's degree from the University of Puget Sound.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was born in Tacoma and attended law school at night while working full time.[^site-about] Under community service, he lists the Kiwanis Club and says he founded the Aktion Club of Federal Way, a service club for adults with developmental disabilities.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists the Judge William Nevins Award in 2014 for commitment to civics education and the DMCJA President's Award for Protecting Judicial Independence in 2018.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Larson emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Judicial independence and nonpartisanship:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Larson says he will "restore trust in our courts by rising above partisan interests, applying the law without fear or favor, and restoring judicial independence."[^sos-pamphlet] He says he "reaches out to Democrats, Republicans, Labor, Business, and voters across Eastern and Western Washington."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Criminal justice:** He says his "criminal justice reforms have been recognized nationwide" and that he has worked with legislators of both parties "to help our trial courts address crime and addiction with compassion and accountability."[^sos-pamphlet] He says the Supreme Court's decisions "have weakened that response" and that as a justice he "will help fix current problems."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Applying the law as written:** His website says the role of a justice "is not to impose ideology" but to ensure "the law is applied as written, consistently, and without political pressure from any direction."[^site]
- **Court rulemaking:** His website says the Supreme Court sets the rules of procedure for the state's courts, including bail rules, public defense caseload limits, and bar licensing standards.[^site] It says this authority "demands justices who have experienced the effects of these rules first-hand."[^site]
- **Trial court experience:** He says the court "needs Dave's unique perspective gained from his significant trial judge experience."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Individual rights:** He says he "will protect your individual rights and the judicial independence that is currently under threat."[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Larson lists the Kiwanis Club and the Aktion Club of Federal Way, which he says he founded.[^sos-pamphlet] His website lists membership in the Washington State Bar Association, King County Bar Association, and District and Municipal Court Judges Association, and service in the Civil Air Patrol.[^site-record] He says he was rated "Exceptionally Well Qualified" as a judge and Supreme Court candidate and "AV Preeminent" as a trial lawyer.[^sos-pamphlet] His pamphlet statement does not list endorsements, and his website does not have an endorsements page.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

## Record

The pamphlet lists Larson as a retired trial judge with 18 years on the bench and as a former president of the Federal Way School Board.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he took the bench at the Federal Way Municipal Court in 2008 at the city's request and was re-elected four times, retiring in 2025.[^site-about] His website also says he ran for this Supreme Court seat in 2024, received more than 1.6 million votes, and lost by 0.61 percent.[^site]

## Funding

As of October 16, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Larson's campaign had raised $436,783.67 and spent $365,295.31.[^finance] About 12.3 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The largest single line in the filings is $37,050.50 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors, each giving $4,800, are Steve Gordon (retired), Debra Blodgett (retired), the Council of Police Political Support, and Melissa Gordon (retired).[^finance]
