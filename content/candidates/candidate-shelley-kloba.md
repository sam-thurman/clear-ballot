## Background

Shelley Kloba is the state representative for Legislative District 1, Position 2, and is listed as a Democrat on her official legislature page.[^leg-member] She is vice chair of the House Technology, Economic Development, & Veterans Committee.[^leg-member] She also serves on the Consumer Protection & Business and Environment & Energy committees and on the joint Gambling Commission.[^leg-member] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Kloba says she has represented the district since 2017 and served on the Kirkland City Council from 2013 to 2016.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she founded the House Technology caucus and serves on a National Conference of State Legislatures task force on artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and data privacy.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she has been a licensed health care provider for over 20 years.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists a bachelor of science in kinesiology from the University of Illinois, a certificate in energy policy planning from the University of Idaho, and the Seattle Massage School.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists the Kirkland Kiwanis Club as a current activity, and says she was a Lake Washington Schools Foundation trustee, Kirkland Park Board member, and Washington State PTA legislative director.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website says she got her start in advocacy through her local PTA after her daughter started school.[^site]

## Positions

- **Data privacy and artificial intelligence:** In the pamphlet, Kloba says she has fought to improve data privacy, rein in data brokers, and reduce the harms of AI on consumers.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she is working to create a data broker registry and has sponsored the People's Privacy Act.[^site-issues] It says that bill would let consumers opt out of AI profiling in decisions like job interviews and insurance rates.[^site-issues]
- **Data centers:** Her website says the economic benefits of data centers must be weighed against heat, noise, water use, and strain on energy transmission and generation.[^site-issues]
- **Taxes and affordability:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work to fix what she calls the state's regressive tax code and cut costs for working families.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports the millionaires tax passed last session and the capital gains tax as a start toward correcting what she calls an "upside down" tax code.[^site-issues]
- **Education:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work to fully fund education.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says funding the education system in compliance with the McCleary decision, the state Supreme Court ruling on school funding, should be at the top of the agenda.[^site-issues]
- **Transportation:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work to improve transportation infrastructure.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website calls for more frequent and reliable bus options, planning for rail, faster highway incident response, and more affordable housing near jobs.[^site-issues]
- **Health care:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work to increase access to behavioral health care.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says access to health care should not be tied to employment and points to the regional crisis response system in the 1st District as a model.[^site-issues]
- **Housing:** Her website says she worked to secure over $5 million in the budget toward low-income housing in Kenmore and supports a variety of housing types at every income level.[^site-issues]
- **Environment:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work to create green jobs.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports reducing dependence on fossil fuels, funding waterway monitoring, and workforce retraining programs.[^site-issues]
- **Civil rights:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work to protect civil rights.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Kloba's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Democratic Party".[^kc-sample-ballot] In the pamphlet, she lists self-reported endorsements from the Snohomish County Democrats, Sierra Club, Washington State Labor Council, Washington Education Association PAC, Washington Alliance for Gun Responsibility, and Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website lists self-reported endorsements from organizations including the 1st Legislative District Democrats, AFT Washington, IAM 751, IBEW Local 77, UFCW 3000, Washington Conservation Action, and the Washington State Council of Fire Fighters.[^site-endorsements] The website lists more organizations, as well as state legislators including Speaker Laurie Jinkins, Sen. Derek Stanford, and Rep. Davina Duerr, and city officials from Kenmore, Bothell, Lake Forest Park, Woodinville, and Kirkland.[^site-endorsements] In the pamphlet, Kloba says she is an ex-officio member of the Washington State Gambling Commission and a member of the Kirkland Kiwanis Club.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Record

Kloba was the prime sponsor of 16 bills and resolutions in the 2025-26 term, according to the legislature's bills-by-sponsor report.[^leg-sponsored] Two became law: 2SHB 1024, on St. Edward State Park, and SHB 2152, on cannabis use in health facilities.[^leg-sponsored] Other bills she sponsored include HB 1671 on personal data privacy, HB 1887 and HB 2483 on a data broker registry, and HB 1984 on autonomous vehicles.[^leg-sponsored] On the shortlist bills, Kloba voted yea on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parental-rights law created by Initiative 2081.[^leg-member] She voted yea on ESSB 6346 (2026), the bill creating the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million.[^leg-member] She voted yea on ESSB 5167, the 2025-27 operating budget, and on ESSB 5998, the 2026 supplemental operating budget.[^leg-member] She voted yea on the 2025 revenue bills ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax changes) and ESSB 5814 (sales tax on certain services).[^leg-member] She also voted yea on ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax surcharges), ESSB 5794 (repeal of certain tax preferences), and SHB 2077 (tax on certain zero-emission vehicle credit sales).[^leg-member] HB 1699, SB 5097, and SB 5012, the 2025 bills on girls' interscholastic sports, died in committee without a floor vote, so there is no vote to report.[^leg-member]

## Funding

According to PDC filings, Kloba's campaign had raised $71,993.94 and spent $48,838.72 as of October 15, 2026.[^finance] About 5.8% of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums campaigns report as small contributions.[^finance] The largest line item in the filings is $10,000 listed as "Miscellaneous Receipts", with no employer or occupation filed.[^finance] The next largest contributors, at $2,400 each, are the Justice for All PAC, the Credit Union Legislative Action Fund, Delta Dental, and the Nisqually Indian Tribe.[^finance]
