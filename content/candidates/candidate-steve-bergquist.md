## Background

Steve Bergquist is the state representative for the 11th Legislative District, Position 2.[^leg-member] The Legislature's member page lists him as a Democrat and as chair of the House Early Learning & Human Services Committee.[^leg-member] It also lists him on the Appropriations and Education committees.[^leg-member] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Bergquist says he is a history and social studies teacher in the Renton School District.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he formerly owned Aces Tennis, a small business that ran the City of Renton's tennis programs.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists Hazen High School in Renton, a bachelor's degree in political science and history from the University of Washington, and a master's in teaching from Western Washington University.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists board service with Special Olympics Washington, the Renton Historical Society & Genealogy Center, and the Renton Regional Community Foundation.[^sos-pamphlet] On his campaign website, Bergquist says he teaches at Lindbergh High School, is a member of the Renton Education Association, and that his family has lived in the community for four generations.[^site] The website says he served six years as vice chair of the House Appropriations Committee and coaches tennis and golf at Lindbergh High School.[^site-education]

## Positions

- **Child care and preschool:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Bergquist says he will continue to expand programs so working families can afford child care and preschool.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says child care "must be made affordable and accessible for all" and that universal preschool would improve education in the state.[^site-childcare]
- **College affordability:** In the pamphlet, he says he will expand college promise programs like the Renton and Tukwila Promise.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was prime sponsor of HB 1587, which provides state matching funds for local promise programs.[^site-education]
- **Housing:** In the pamphlet, he says he will help create more housing so people can afford to live near where they work.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **K-12 education:** His website says he believes an educator should represent the district and notes that K-12 education makes up more than half of the state budget.[^site-education]
- **Small business:** His website says he believes in "an atmosphere that encourages small business" and in streamlining regulations for small business owners.[^site-jobs]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Bergquist's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Democratic Party."[^kc-sample-ballot] The Legislature's member page lists his party as Democrat.[^leg-member] His website says he is a member of the Renton Education Association and a past member of the Renton Chamber of Commerce and the Southwest King County Chamber of Commerce.[^site][^site-jobs] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Bergquist self-reports endorsements from "teachers, nurses, your firefighters, the 11th District Democrats and many more."[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website menu does not include an endorsements page.[^site]

## Record

The Legislature lists Bergquist as prime sponsor, meaning the legislator who introduced the measure, of 16 bills and resolutions in the 2025-26 session.[^leg-sponsored] They include 2SHB 1587 on partner promise scholarships, ESHB 2049 on K-12 education funding, and SHB 2714 on the food assistance caseload.[^leg-sponsored] Those three became law; HR 4687, a resolution honoring Steve Nelsen, was adopted, and the remaining 12 bills did not pass the House.[^leg-sponsored]

On the shortlist bills, Bergquist voted yea (yes) on ESHB 1296, the bill amending the parental-rights statute created by Initiative 2081.[^leg-member] He voted yea on ESSB 6346, the bill creating the 9.9 percent tax on income over $1 million.[^leg-member] He voted yea on ESSB 5167, the 2025-27 operating budget, and on ESSB 5998, the 2026 supplemental operating budget.[^leg-member] He voted yea on all five 2025 revenue bills.[^leg-member] Those were ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax changes), ESSB 5814 (sales tax on certain services), and ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax surcharges).[^leg-member] They also included ESSB 5794 (repeal of certain tax preferences) and SHB 2077 (tax on certain zero-emission vehicle credit sales).[^leg-member] HB 1699, SB 5097, and SB 5012, the girls' sports bills, died in committee and never received a floor vote.[^leg-member]

## Funding

According to Public Disclosure Commission filings, Bergquist's campaign had raised $185,123.58 and spent $163,320.27 as of October 13, 2026.[^finance] About 0.4 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus unitemized gifts of $25 or less.[^finance] The largest single entry in the filings is $112,873.58 listed as "miscellaneous receipts."[^finance] The top named contributors, at $2,400 each, are BECU, Delta Dental of Washington, the Credit Union Legislative Action Fund (Northwest Credit Union Association), and the Kent Firefighters IAFF Local 1747 PAC.[^finance]
