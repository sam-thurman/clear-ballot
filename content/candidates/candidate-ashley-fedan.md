## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Ashley Fedan says she has more than 25 years of experience as a registered nurse and Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist, a nurse trained to give anesthesia.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she owns a small business providing anesthesia care, including gender-affirming care, abortion, and reproductive care.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she is a Navy veteran who served as a lieutenant commander in the Nurse Corps.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists a nursing degree from Case Western Reserve University, a master's degree from York College of Pennsylvania, and a doctorate in education from Creighton University.[^sos-pamphlet] On her campaign website, Fedan says she was born and raised in West Virginia and began her nursing career at the Cleveland Clinic in 1999.[^site-about] She says she joined the Navy Nurse Corps in 2012, started her anesthesia business in 2013, and lives in Fairwood.[^site-about] In the pamphlet, she lists community roles with the Pro-Choice Washington PAC, APRNs of Washington State, the Washington Association of Nurse Anesthesiology, and Southend Indivisible.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

- **Health care:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Fedan says she will "fight for accessible healthcare" and notes that no nurses currently serve in the Legislature.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she will push for transparency and accountability from insurance companies and hospital systems and for training more nurses and health care workers.[^site-priorities]
- **Housing:** Her website says she will work to cut bureaucratic delays that slow affordable housing projects and to build partnerships between the state and nonprofit housing builders.[^site-priorities]
- **Wages and workers:** She says she will push for wages that keep up with the cost of living and will stand behind workers seeking fair treatment and safe conditions.[^site-priorities]
- **Education and jobs:** In the pamphlet, she says she will fight for K-12 investments, career training, and well-paying jobs.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Cost of living:** She says working people are "squeezed by housing and childcare costs, healthcare bills, and education debt."[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Fedan's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Democratic Party."[^kc-sample-ballot] In the Voters' Pamphlet, she lists herself as a precinct committee officer for the 11th Legislative District, a 2024 Democratic National Committee delegate, and former chairwoman of the 28th Legislative District Democrats.[^sos-pamphlet] In the pamphlet, she self-reports endorsements from "Nurses, State Employees, Elected Officials, 11th LD Dems, County Dems, Planned Parenthood, more."[^sos-pamphlet] Her website names four endorsing legislators: Senator Manka Dhingra and Representatives Jamila Taylor, Julia Reed, and My-Linh Thai.[^site-endorsements] The website shows its organizational endorsements only as logo images, so they are not named here.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

Fedan is not an incumbent.[^kc-sample-ballot] In the Voters' Pamphlet, she lists her elected experience as precinct committee officer, Democratic National Committee delegate, and former 28th Legislative District precinct committee officer and chairwoman.[^sos-pamphlet] She does not list any legislative or other government office.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

According to Public Disclosure Commission filings, Fedan's campaign had raised $91,828.23 and spent $99,937.10 as of October 13, 2026.[^finance] About 20.9 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus unitemized gifts of $25 or less.[^finance] The largest entry in the filings is $15,732.34 in "small contributions," the lump sum campaigns report for unitemized gifts of $25 or less.[^finance] The top named contributors are Jennifer Tanguay ($3,000; self-employed CRNA) and Sean Howley, Kathleen Fedan, and Jeffrey Fedan ($2,400 each; each listed as not employed).[^finance]
