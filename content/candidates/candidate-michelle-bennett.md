## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Michelle Bennett says she was police chief in Maple Valley, Sammamish, and Mercer Island.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she was a planning and operations major in the King County Sheriff's Office and a sheriff's deputy in North Bend.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she has been an instructor at Northwestern University and Bellevue College.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists a doctorate in education from Seattle Pacific University.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists a Master of Science in organizational development and a Bachelor of Arts in law and justice from Central Washington University.[^sos-pamphlet] Under community service, she says she co-created the "Take a Stand Against Bullying" curriculum and created programs including the Sammamish Healthy Communities Coalitions and youth explorer and volunteer programs.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website says she is running as a Republican "to reflect her fiscal values".[^site]

## Positions

- **Taxes:** In the pamphlet, Bennett says Olympia recently voted for what she calls the largest tax increase in state history while families struggle with rising costs.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she will oppose efforts to expand Washington's income tax.[^site]
- **State spending:** In the pamphlet, she says she will ensure state spending delivers measurable results.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she will push for disciplined budgeting, funding core priorities first, and eliminating waste and duplication.[^site-priorities]
- **Public safety:** In the pamphlet, she says police have been left without clear tools to protect victims and neighborhoods.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports law enforcement, firefighters, and emergency responders, and wants to address property crime and public disorder.[^site-priorities]
- **Schools:** In the pamphlet, she says she will fight for stronger schools.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports student achievement, classroom discipline, expanded trades and apprenticeship pathways, and parents as partners in education.[^site-priorities]
- **Housing and cost of living:** Her website says she will support reducing regulatory costs that make housing more expensive and housing solutions that fit local communities.[^site-priorities]
- **Business:** Her website says she will oppose new tax burdens on local businesses and reduce red tape for local employers.[^site-priorities]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Bennett's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Republican Party".[^kc-sample-ballot] The King County Republican Party lists Bennett among its endorsed candidates on its website.[^endorse-1] Her campaign website lists self-reported endorsements from elected officials including King County Councilmember Reagan Dunn, Rep. Brian Burnett, Sen. Drew MacEwen, and Enumclaw Mayor Anthony Wright.[^site-endorsements] It also lists former officials including former Congressman Dave Reichert, former Rep. Chad Magendanz, and former Secretary of State Sam Reed, and individuals including Maple Valley Police Chief DJ Nesel.[^site-endorsements] The website's "Organizations" heading did not list any organizations when this site checked it.[^site-endorsements] In the pamphlet, Bennett says she has been president of the King County Police Chiefs/Sheriffs and the Washington State FBI National Academy Associates.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists the Washington State Police Chiefs' and Sheriffs' Executive Board, legislative committee co-chair, and the Northwestern University Alumni Board.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Record

Under "Elected Experience", the pamphlet lists president of the King County Police Chiefs/Sheriffs, the Washington State FBI National Academy Associates, and the Washington State Police Chiefs' and Sheriffs' Executive Board.[^sos-pamphlet] It also lists legislative committee co-chair and the Northwestern University Alumni Board.[^sos-pamphlet] The pamphlet does not list a government elected office.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

According to PDC filings, Bennett's campaign had raised $32,310.22 and spent $25,975.77 as of October 13, 2026.[^finance] About 15.3% of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums campaigns report as small contributions.[^finance] The largest contributor is the House Republican Organizational Committee at $6,000.[^finance] Next are Kemper Holdings LLC at $2,400 and Roger White, listed as retired, at $2,000.[^finance] The King County Police Officers Guild PAC and the Council of Police Political Support each gave $1,200.[^finance]
