## Background

Zach Hall is the state representative for Legislative District 5, Position 1, and is listed as a Democrat on his official legislature page.[^leg-member] He is vice chair of the House Environment & Energy Committee and serves on the Local Government and Transportation committees and the Joint Select Committee on Civic Health.[^leg-member] His campaign website says he was appointed to the House in June 2025.[^site] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Hall says he is a former Issaquah City Councilmember, and his website says he served on the council for six years.[^sos-pamphlet][^site] He says he was a legislative assistant for the state House and previously consulted for cities and state agencies.[^sos-pamphlet] He lists a Master of Public Administration from the University of Washington Evans School of Public Policy & Governance and a Bachelor of Science from the University of Washington.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he chaired the Eastside Fire & Rescue Board and serves on the Friends of the Issaquah Salmon Hatchery Board.[^sos-pamphlet] He describes himself as a lifelong Issaquah resident.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

- **Energy costs:** In the pamphlet, Hall says he sponsored and passed bipartisan bills to lower energy costs in his first session.[^sos-pamphlet] His website points to House Bill 2296 on utility energy-efficiency investments and House Bill 2575 on utility reporting requirements.[^site]
- **Transportation:** In the pamphlet, he says he prioritized safety and congestion improvements along SR 18.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he wants to protect funding to expand State Route 18 and supports transit access, connected bike networks, and pedestrian safety grants.[^site-priorities]
- **Housing and cost of living:** In the pamphlet, he says he supported reforms to fix the housing crisis.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says the state needs more affordable housing and reforms that make it easier to finance and build homes faster.[^site-priorities]
- **Schools:** In the pamphlet, he says he is committed to fully funding schools.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he grew up attending Issaquah public schools.[^site-priorities]
- **Public safety:** In the pamphlet, he says he is committed to supporting first responders.[^sos-pamphlet] His website lists wildfire and flood preparation, preventing gun violence, and behavioral health and substance use interventions as parts of public safety.[^site-priorities]
- **Childcare and health care:** In the pamphlet, he says he will push for affordable childcare and healthcare.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Environment and energy grid:** His website says he is committed to salmon recovery, habitat restoration, and reducing greenhouse gas emissions, and calls for investment in transmission infrastructure to modernize the power grid.[^site-priorities]
- **Small business:** In the pamphlet, he says he is committed to helping small businesses thrive.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Hall's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Democratic Party".[^kc-sample-ballot] In the pamphlet, he lists self-reported endorsements from Eastside Firefighters, the WA Labor Council, the WA Education Association, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates, and the Alliance for Gun Responsibility.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists Congresswoman Kim Schrier, State Sen. Victoria Hunt, State Rep. Lisa Callan, and local mayors.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website lists self-reported endorsements from organizations including the Affordable Housing Council, WA Conservation Action, Sierra Club WA State, WA Realtors, and the WA Farm Bureau.[^site-endorsements] It also lists the 5th Legislative District Democrats, the Snoqualmie Tribe, and SEIU 1199NW.[^site-endorsements] The website lists more organizations and labor unions, as well as Gov. Bob Ferguson, Public Lands Commissioner Dave Upthegrove, King County Executive Girmay Zahilay, and state and local officials.[^site-endorsements] In the pamphlet, Hall says he serves on the Friends of the Issaquah Salmon Hatchery Board.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Record

Hall was the prime sponsor of nine bills in the 2025-26 term, according to the legislature's bills-by-sponsor report.[^leg-sponsored] Three became law: HB 2272 on ski areas and winter sports, SHB 2296 on distributed energy resources, and EHB 2575 on environmental reporting.[^leg-sponsored] Other bills he sponsored include HB 2277 on wildfire risk models and HB 2421 on 6PPD and substitutes in tires.[^leg-sponsored] On the shortlist bills, Hall voted yea on ESSB 6346 (2026), the bill creating the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million.[^leg-member] He voted yea on ESSB 5998, the 2026 supplemental operating budget.[^leg-member] Hall's vote is not recorded on ESHB 1296, ESSB 5167, ESSB 5813, ESSB 5814, ESHB 2081, ESSB 5794, or SHB 2077, which the House passed in April 2025.[^leg-member] His campaign website says he was appointed to the House in June 2025, after those votes.[^site] HB 1699, SB 5097, and SB 5012, the 2025 bills on girls' interscholastic sports, died in committee without a floor vote, so there is no vote to report.[^leg-member]

## Funding

According to PDC filings, Hall's campaign had raised $135,035.57 and spent $100,083.03 as of October 13, 2026.[^finance] About 3.4% of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums campaigns report as small contributions.[^finance] The largest contributor is the House Democratic Campaign Committee at $40,227.38.[^finance] The filings also list a lump sum of $3,887.06 as "Small Contributions".[^finance] The next largest contributors are the SEIU 1199NW United for Quality Care PAC at $2,650, Delta Dental of Washington at $2,400, and Council 28 AFSCME Washington Federation of State Employees at $2,400.[^finance]
