## Background

Lisa Callan is the state representative for Legislative District 5, Position 2, and is listed as a Democrat on her official legislature page.[^leg-member] She is vice chair of the House Capital Budget Committee and serves on the Appropriations and Education committees.[^leg-member] She also serves on joint committees including the Children and Youth Behavioral Health Work Group and the Capital Projects Advisory Review Board.[^leg-member] In the Voters' Pamphlet, Callan says she is a former Issaquah School Board director.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website says she served two terms on the school board, including a stint as president, and was first elected to the House in 2018.[^site] She says she was a lead engineer and project manager for Boeing and a consultant managing large e-commerce software development programs.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists a Bachelor of Science in mathematics with an emphasis in computer science from Northern Arizona University.[^sos-pamphlet] She says she is a member of Kiwanis and the Issaquah Chamber of Commerce.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists past roles as a PTA leader, a loaned executive to United Way of King County, and a board member of The Garage Teen Center and Influence the Choice.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she and her husband raised their son in the Issaquah Highlands.[^site]

## Positions

- **Public schools:** In the pamphlet, Callan says she will keep fighting for great public schools.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says her HB 2180 raised the cap on special education funding and that she helped negotiate $1 billion for K-12 construction in the 2025-27 capital budget.[^site]
- **Youth mental health:** In the pamphlet, she says she will fight for mental health care for kids.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she wrote HB 1580 to find homes for kids in psychiatric crisis stuck in hospital emergency rooms and co-chairs the state's Children & Youth Behavioral Work Group.[^site]
- **Housing:** In the pamphlet, she says she will fight for workforce housing for families who serve their communities.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she supports accessory dwelling units, lot splitting, and density along transit corridors.[^site]
- **Gun safety:** In the pamphlet, she says she supports gun safety measures that save lives.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she was prime sponsor of permit-to-purchase, lost-or-stolen reporting, and bulk firearm purchase limit bills.[^site]
- **Reproductive freedom:** In the pamphlet, she says she will defend reproductive freedom.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Childcare:** In the pamphlet, she says she will fight for affordable childcare.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says her bills helped lock in roughly $100 million for childcare construction.[^site]
- **Environment:** In the pamphlet, she says she will protect forests and clean air.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she voted for the Climate Commitment Act and campaigned against the 2024 initiative to repeal it.[^site]
- **Taxes:** In the pamphlet, she says she will work for a tax code that "doesn't squeeze working people".[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Online safety and AI:** Her website says she was prime sponsor of HB 2225, which it says regulates AI companion chatbots, including content filters and disclosures for minors.[^site]
- **Workers:** Her website says she supports apprenticeship requirements on public works, prevailing wage, and project labor agreements.[^site]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Callan's party preference on the ballot is "Prefers Democratic Party".[^kc-sample-ballot] In the pamphlet, she lists self-reported endorsements from educators, the State Labor Council, grocery workers, firefighters, nurses, Sierra Club, and Washington Conservation Action.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists Planned Parenthood, the Alliance for Gun Responsibility, building trades, and the Housing Alliance.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists Congresswoman Kim Schrier and mayors and council members across the 5th District.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website lists self-reported endorsements from the Washington Education Association, Eastside Firefighters Local 1762, Iron Workers Local 86, Sheet Metal Workers Local 66, and Operating Engineers Local 302.[^site] It also lists King County Democrats and the Affordable Housing Council.[^site] The website also lists State Sen. Victoria Hunt, King County Councilmember Sarah Perry, and the mayors of Issaquah, Snoqualmie, North Bend, and Maple Valley.[^site] In the pamphlet, Callan says she is a member of Kiwanis and the Issaquah Chamber of Commerce.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Record

Callan was the prime sponsor of 19 bills in the 2025-26 term, according to the legislature's bills-by-sponsor report.[^leg-sponsored] Ten became law, including SHB 1272 on the children in crisis program, HB 1314 on early learning facilities, ESHB 2225 on AI companion chatbots, and 2SHB 2429 on youth behavioral health.[^leg-sponsored] Others that became law include ESHB 1795 on student restraint and isolation, HB 1796 on school construction debt, and HB 2338 on community weatherization.[^leg-sponsored] On the shortlist bills, Callan voted yea on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parental-rights law created by Initiative 2081.[^leg-member] She voted yea on ESSB 6346 (2026), the bill creating the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million.[^leg-member] She voted yea on ESSB 5167, the 2025-27 operating budget, and on ESSB 5998, the 2026 supplemental operating budget.[^leg-member] She voted yea on the 2025 revenue bills ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax changes) and ESSB 5814 (sales tax on certain services).[^leg-member] She also voted yea on ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax surcharges), ESSB 5794 (repeal of certain tax preferences), and SHB 2077 (tax on certain zero-emission vehicle credit sales).[^leg-member] HB 1699, SB 5097, and SB 5012, the 2025 bills on girls' interscholastic sports, died in committee without a floor vote, so there is no vote to report.[^leg-member]

## Funding

According to PDC filings, Callan's campaign had raised $91,222.08 and spent $84,143.40 as of October 13, 2026.[^finance] About 2.2% of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums campaigns report as small contributions.[^finance] The largest contributor is the Washington Senate Democratic Campaign (WSDC) at $7,656.[^finance] The next largest, at $2,400 each, are the IUPAT Political Action Together Legislative Education Committee, Hospitals for a Healthy Future PAC, Delta Dental of Washington, and the League of Education Voters.[^finance]
