## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Todd A. Bloom says he has spent more than two decades providing legal and professional services to companies, executives, business owners, and Washington families.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he served as an arbitrator for FINRA, the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, from 1999 to 2020; an arbitrator decides disputes outside of court.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he spent more than a decade as a tax attorney and senior manager in major accounting firms and was an active-duty Navy officer from 2002 to 2013.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website says he retired from the Navy as a lieutenant commander in 2018 and that he and his wife live in Tacoma.[^site-about] He lists a master of laws degree from the University of Washington, law and business degrees from Tulane University, a master's degree from the Naval War College, and a bachelor's degree from Simpson College.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists CPA, CFA, and CFP professional designations.[^sos-pamphlet] Under community service, he lists the Washington State Bar Association Board of Governors, the Nonprofit Finance Council, and adjunct teaching at the University of Washington and Tulane.[^sos-pamphlet] According to Ballotpedia, Bloom ran for Washington Supreme Court Position 2 in 2024 and for the U.S. House in Washington's 6th District in 2016 and 2022.[^news-1]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Bloom emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Applying the law impartially:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Bloom says that if elected he promises "to uphold and apply the law impartially and fairly."[^sos-pamphlet] His website says judges "should faithfully apply the law as written, uphold the Constitution, and ensure every person who enters the courtroom receives a fair and impartial hearing."[^site-about]
- **New perspective on the court:** He says this election "is a choice--whether to retain the current justices or to give someone new your vote."[^sos-pamphlet] He says he represents "meaningful new perspective, experience, and common sense which will bring balance to the court."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Military service:** He says his recall to active duty after 9/11 and deployment to the Persian Gulf shaped his "understanding of duty, accountability, and leadership under pressure."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Problem solving:** He says he has "worked to solve complex problems thoughtfully, collaboratively, and fairly" throughout his career.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Experience with everyday people:** He says his background "has given me a deep appreciation for hard work, sacrifice, and the experiences of everyday people."[^sos-pamphlet] He says he worked farm, grocery, and restaurant jobs to pay for school.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Decision-making experience:** His website says his work as a FINRA arbitrator and as a Navy officer overseeing disciplinary matters "strengthened his ability to evaluate evidence, apply governing rules, and make fair, impartial decisions."[^site-about]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Bloom lists service on the Washington State Bar Association Board of Governors and the Nonprofit Finance Council.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he served on the Board of Governors for the 2023 to 2026 term and was re-elected to a second term through September 2029.[^site-about] His website says he is admitted to the Washington and Louisiana state bars, the U.S. Tax Court, and two federal district courts.[^site-about] His pamphlet statement does not list endorsements, and his website does not have an endorsements page.[^sos-pamphlet][^site] Ballotpedia reports that it did not identify endorsements for Bloom in this election.[^news-1]

## Record

The pamphlet lists no elected office for Bloom.[^sos-pamphlet] According to Ballotpedia, he ran in the 2024 nonpartisan primary for Supreme Court Position 2, finishing third with 16.3 percent of the vote, and did not advance.[^news-1] Ballotpedia also reports he ran as a Republican for the U.S. House in Washington's 6th District in 2016, losing in the general election, and in 2022, when he did not advance from the primary.[^news-1] In the pamphlet, Bloom says he was encouraged to seek election to the Supreme Court while serving on the state bar's Board of Governors.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

As of October 15, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Bloom's campaign had raised $32,239.46 and spent $11,549.10.[^finance] About 8.7 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The top named contributors are Collin Hathaway ($4,800, owner, Skylight Capital), Todd A. Bloom himself ($3,343.38), Connie Kearney ($2,400, retired), Goodman Real Estate Inc. ($2,400), and Lee Kearney ($2,400, retired).[^finance]
