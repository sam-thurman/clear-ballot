## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Jaime Michelle Hawk describes herself as a Superior Court judge, former federal public defender, and former ACLU civil rights attorney with more than 20 years of legal work.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website says she serves as a King County Superior Court judge.[^site-experience] She says she was raised by a single mother, was the first in her family to attend college, and graduated with honors from Gonzaga University School of Law.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she also graduated with honors from Gonzaga University and worked with survivors of domestic violence and sexual assault before law school.[^site-experience] The website says that after law school she was an attorney fellow on Senator Ted Kennedy's U.S. Senate Judiciary Committee staff.[^site-experience] She says she has served as an adjunct professor of law and on the Supreme Court Minority and Justice Commission.[^sos-pamphlet] Under community service, she says she has served on boards for immigrant children, voting rights, veterans, and women's rights.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Hawk emphasizes in her pamphlet statement and on her website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Following the law:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Hawk says she has presided over hundreds of criminal, civil, and family law cases and appeals, and that "she follows the law."[^sos-pamphlet] She says she has never had a trial overturned for legal error.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Representing people who cannot afford a lawyer:** She says she "fought in the courtroom for those who could not afford a lawyer" and defended Washingtonians against the federal government.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Independence:** She says she "will bring a strong, independent voice" and "will protect our rights and the rule of law."[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she is committed to "fairness, court independence, and the rule of law."[^site-experience]
- **Women and working families:** She says she "understands the concerns of women and working families and will bring that perspective to the Court."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Court access and bias:** Her website says she serves on a Supreme Court commission "working to eliminate bias in our courts and make them more accessible to working families."[^site]
- **Voting rights and pretrial justice:** Her website says that as a civil rights attorney she helped lead statewide reforms "to improve pretrial justice, reduce bias in the legal system, and expand voting rights."[^site-experience]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Hawk says she serves on the Supreme Court Minority and Justice Commission and was appointed by Senators Murray and Cantwell to their federal judge selection committee.[^sos-pamphlet] She says the American Bar Association selected her to observe Guantanamo Bay military hearings and later to assist families separated at the border.[^sos-pamphlet]

In the pamphlet, Hawk self-reports endorsements from Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal, Governor Bob Ferguson, and more than 100 current and retired judges and justices.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists the National Women's Political Caucus, local and county Democratic organizations, and the State Labor Council.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website's self-reported endorsement list includes the Washington State Labor Council, Washington State Council of Fire Fighters, Planned Parenthood, Washington Conservation Action, and the National Women's Political Caucus of Washington.[^site-endorsements] It also lists the American Federation of Teachers of Washington, UFCW 3000, SEIU Healthcare 775 NW, Washington Education Association, and King County Democrats.[^site-endorsements] The site lists more labor, tribal, and community organizations, Democratic organizations, judges, and elected officials.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

The pamphlet lists Superior Court judge as Hawk's current judicial role; it does not have a separate "Elected Experience" entry for her.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she is a King County Superior Court judge and has issued hundreds of written decisions.[^site-experience] In the pamphlet, Hawk says she has presided over hundreds of criminal, civil, and family law cases, as well as appeals from lower courts.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

As of October 16, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Hawk's campaign had raised $336,702.91 and spent $312,739.15.[^finance] About 7.3 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The filings show $21,381.00 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors are Jaime Hawk herself ($5,000), Jason Amala ($4,800, attorney, Pfau Cochran Vertetis Amala PLLC), Daniel Brodkowitz ($4,800, retired), and Tamara Amala ($4,800, homemaker).[^finance]
