## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Mary Silva says she has 20 years of experience as a clinical audiologist, including seven years as a managing partner.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website describes her as a working mother and full-time health care provider who has worked as an audiologist in the community for 20 years.[^site-about] Silva lists a Doctor of Audiology from Salus University (2009) and a Master of Arts in Audiology/Speech and Hearing Science from Washington State University (2006).[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists a Bachelor of Arts in Communication Science and Disorders from Western Washington University (2003).[^sos-pamphlet] She says she is a former board member of the Washington State Academy of Audiology and has volunteered with Big Brothers Big Sisters and at schools.[^sos-pamphlet] She lists service as a precinct committee officer, a local party position, in Snohomish County since 2022 and in King County in 2008-2012 and 2014-2016.[^sos-pamphlet] She also lists being a delegate to the 2024 Republican State Convention and an alternate in 2008.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Positions

- **Manufacturing and jobs:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Silva says offshoring has stripped away manufacturing capability and she wants policies that give incentives for domestic production.[^sos-pamphlet] Her website says she wants to bring semiconductor manufacturing, IT, AI, and cybersecurity operations back to the United States.[^site-issues]
- **Digital infrastructure and security:** Silva says she will prioritize federal investment to safeguard technology systems, including grids, communications, and private data.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Taxes and corporations:** Silva says she wants to shift economic focus away from Wall Street and billionaires and address offshore tax havens, which she says cost the country trillions in lost revenue.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Small business:** Silva says she will fight for small and medium-sized businesses by lowering regulatory barriers and expanding access to capital for local entrepreneurs.[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Immigration and visas:** Her website says she wants to restructure the H-1B visa program to curb offshoring and encourage hiring of local workers, and to fortify border security.[^site-issues]
- **Workforce training:** Her website says she wants to expand apprenticeships, federal reskilling programs, and trade education as alternatives to four-year degrees.[^site-issues]
- **Defense and intelligence spending:** Her website says she wants a full audit of the Pentagon and an overhaul of the intelligence community to curb contractor spending.[^site-issues]
- **Elections:** Her website says she wants to require voter ID and establish a midweek national voting holiday.[^site-issues]
- **Health care:** Her website says she wants to protect individuals' right to make their own medical decisions without government mandates and guarantee parents authority over their children's health decisions.[^site-issues]
- **Surveillance and privacy:** Her website says she wants to end warrantless domestic surveillance, such as license-plate camera networks, and require warrants before agencies collect location data or communications.[^site-issues]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Silva's ballot listing says "Prefers Republican Party."[^kc-sample-ballot] In the Voters' Pamphlet, she lists service as a Republican precinct committee officer and as a delegate to the 2024 Republican State Convention.[^sos-pamphlet] Her campaign website reports a self-reported endorsement from Stand for Health Freedom.[^site] The Voters' Pamphlet statement does not list any endorsements.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Record

Under "Elected Experience" in the Voters' Pamphlet, Silva lists precinct committee officer posts in Snohomish and King counties and Republican State Convention delegate roles.[^sos-pamphlet] She does not list any public office.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

According to FEC filings, Silva's campaign raised $8,433.70 and spent $7,435.97 through September 30, 2026, and had $1,253.86 in cash on hand.[^finance] About 12.5 percent of receipts came from small donors, meaning unitemized individual contributions of $200 or less.[^finance] The largest contributor listed was the candidate herself, Mary Silva, at $5,629.03, with occupation filed as audiologist and employer filed as "Puget Sound Hearing Aid & Audi" (as written in the filing).[^finance] Other top contributors were Katherine Brennan ($1,000, retired), Allan Geldenhuys ($250, retired), Bill Quantrille ($250, claims clerk at VAMC), and Susan Reade Lund ($250, no employer or occupation filed).[^finance]
