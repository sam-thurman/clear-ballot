## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Ian Birk says he is a judge on the Washington State Court of Appeals, appointed by Governor Jay Inslee and then elected.[^sos-pamphlet] His campaign website says he was appointed to Division I of the Court of Appeals in 2022 and elected without opposition later that year.[^site-about] He says he spent two decades in private practice representing individuals, families, and small businesses in cases involving insurance claims, consumer protection, workplace discrimination, and complex civil litigation.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was most recently a partner at the law firm Keller Rohrback LLP.[^site-about] He lists a bachelor's degree and a law degree from the University of Washington.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he clerked for the Washington State Supreme Court after law school; a law clerk is a lawyer who assists a judge.[^site-about] Under community service, he says he volunteered for more than 10 years with the King County Bar Association Neighborhood Legal Clinics, giving free legal help to people who could not afford an attorney.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he is a fifth-generation Washingtonian and lives in Seattle with his wife.[^site-about]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Birk emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site-values]

- **Judicial independence:** Birk's website says judges "must decide cases based on the law and the facts—never politics, pressure, or popularity."[^site-values] In the Voters' Pamphlet, he says he will "continue authoring opinions that are independent, fair, and grounded in shared values."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Equal access to justice:** He says he has focused on "the right of every person to equal access to justice, equal treatment under the law, and fair outcomes."[^sos-pamphlet] His website says the justice system "must work for everyone, not just those with resources or power."[^site-values]
- **Precedent and clear opinions:** His website says fair outcomes require "careful attention to the record, respect for legal precedent, and thoughtful reasoning," and that decisions should be explained clearly.[^site-values] Precedent means earlier court decisions that guide later ones.[^site-values]
- **Real-world impact:** His website says judicial decisions "affect families, workers, businesses, and communities," and that he approaches judging "with care, humility, and respect for the human consequences."[^site-values]
- **Appellate experience:** He says he has written hundreds of decisions across civil, criminal, family, administrative, and constitutional law.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he is "the only candidate in this position with statewide judicial experience."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Representing everyday people:** He says his background "representing everyday Washingtonians" will bring "a unique perspective to the Supreme Court."[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] In the pamphlet, Birk lists more than 10 years of service with the King County Bar Association Neighborhood Legal Clinics.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he was rated "exceptionally well qualified" by Washington Women Lawyers and the Cardozo Society.[^sos-pamphlet]

In the pamphlet, Birk self-reports endorsements from Attorney General Nick Brown, Justices González, Gordon McCloud, Whitener, Montoya-Lewis, Mungia, and Angelis, and former Justices Ireland and Yu.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists U.S. Representative Pramila Jayapal, King County Executive Girmay Zahilay, the Washington State Labor Council, Machinists, Teachers, King County Democrats, and more than 100 judges.[^sos-pamphlet] His website's self-reported endorsement list adds Governor Bob Ferguson, former Governor Jay Inslee, Justice Colleen Melody, and Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates.[^site-endorsements] The site lists more judges, state legislators, local officials, and community members.[^site-endorsements]

## Record

The pamphlet lists Birk as a judge on the State Court of Appeals, appointed by Governor Inslee and then elected.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he was appointed in 2022, elected later that year, and has authored more than 200 opinions while sitting in all three divisions of the Court of Appeals.[^site-about] The pamphlet does not have a separate "Elected Experience" entry for him.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

As of October 16, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Birk's campaign had raised $577,705.38 and spent $282,088.42.[^finance] About 2 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The filings show $10,073.00 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors, each giving $4,800, are Jason Baker (attorney, Law Offices of Jason Baker, PLLC), Carly Roberts (retired), Ian Bauer (attorney, Pfau Cochran Vertetis Amala), and Lucas Garrett (attorney, Schroeter Goldmark & Bender).[^finance]
