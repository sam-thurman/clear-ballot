## Background

In the Voters' Pamphlet, Scott Edwards lists work at the law firms Ballard Spahr/Lane Powell since 2010, Perkins Coie from 1999 to 2010, and Bogle & Gates from 1996 to 1999.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he served as a practice chair at Ballard Spahr/Lane Powell from 2010 to 2024 and on its board in 2017 to 2019 and 2023 to 2024.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he has been an adjunct professor at the University of Washington School of Law since 2002, teaching state and local tax.[^sos-pamphlet][^site-about] He lists a law degree with honors from the University of Washington School of Law in 1991 and an economics degree, magna cum laude, from Princeton University in 1988.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he grew up in Anchorage, Alaska, where his father served as Alaska Attorney General from 1968 to 1970 and as U.S. Attorney from 1971 to 1978.[^site-about] Under community service, the pamphlet lists the American Red Cross, St. Cecilia Parish, Scouting America, Bainbridge Island Youth Orchestra, and Kids in Concert.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says he served on the board of the Western Washington chapters of the American Red Cross from 2006 to 2015.[^site-about]

## Positions

Washington's Code of Judicial Conduct bars judges and judicial candidates from making pledges, promises, or commitments on issues likely to come before the court.[^cjc] The bullets below summarize the themes Edwards emphasizes in his pamphlet statement and on his website, not policy positions.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

- **Role of the court:** In the Voters' Pamphlet, Edwards says the role of the Supreme Court "is to apply the constitution and laws as written, not rewrite them."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Independence:** He says he is running "to bring independence, principled judgment, and respect for the rule of law" to the court.[^sos-pamphlet] His website says his decisions would be "guided by the law and the record — never by politics or pressure."[^site]
- **Predictability:** He says Washington "deserves justices who will apply the law predictably, as written, and without favoring any political agenda."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Precedent and process:** His website says he believes the court "must remain a place where decisions are grounded in the Constitution, statutes, and precedent."[^site-about] Precedent means earlier court decisions that guide later ones.[^site-court]
- **Accountability:** He says his commitment is "to protect your constitutional rights and work to make the Washington Supreme Court accountable and worthy of your trust."[^sos-pamphlet]
- **Experience:** He says he has represented clients at all levels of Washington's court system for more than 30 years, including more than a dozen Washington Supreme Court cases, as a tax and constitutional attorney.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Affiliations and endorsements

Supreme Court justice is a nonpartisan office, so no party preference appears on the ballot for this race.[^kc-sample-ballot] The Code of Judicial Conduct also bars judicial candidates from publicly identifying as a member of a political organization, except as needed to vote.[^cjc] His website says he values public service as independent counsel "unattached to political parties or organizations."[^site-about] In the pamphlet, Edwards lists membership in the Washington State Bar Association, the American Bar Association, and the Council on State Taxation.[^sos-pamphlet] He also lists the American Red Cross, St. Cecilia Parish, Scouting America, Bainbridge Island Youth Orchestra, and Kids in Concert under community service.[^sos-pamphlet] His pamphlet statement does not list endorsements, and his website does not have an endorsements page.[^sos-pamphlet][^site]

## Record

The pamphlet's experience sections list no elected office for Edwards.[^sos-pamphlet] He says he has worked at law firms since 1996 and has represented clients in more than a dozen Washington Supreme Court cases.[^sos-pamphlet] Position 1 is on the ballot for a two-year unexpired term, meaning the remainder of the term of the justice who left the seat.[^sos-pamphlet]

## Funding

As of October 16, 2026, Public Disclosure Commission filings show Edwards's campaign had raised $180,173.68 and spent $154,240.59.[^finance] About 4.6 percent of contribution dollars came from small donors, meaning gifts of $100 or less plus the lump sums the campaign reports as "small contributions."[^finance] The filings show $5,155.00 in small contributions.[^finance] The top named contributors, each giving $4,800, are HMG LLC, Barry Abbott (retired), Steve Gordon (retired), and Patricia B. Heller (retired).[^finance]
