## What it does

Washington has a law, RCW 28A.605.005, that lists rights of parents and legal guardians of public-school children, such as the right to see their child's school records and to be told about their child's academic performance.[^explanatory] That list was first put in law in 2024 by Initiative 2081, and in 2025 the Legislature changed it; many of the rights on the list also exist in other state and federal laws.[^explanatory] This initiative would undo all of the 2025 changes and put the list back exactly as it was enacted in 2024.[^explanatory][^fulltext] It is an "Initiative to the Legislature," meaning it was first sent to the Legislature, which chose to let voters decide it.[^pamphlet]

## What changes if it passes / if it fails

If it passes, the 2024 version of the law returns, with these main differences from current law as described by the Attorney General.[^explanatory] Schools would have to produce a child's records within 10 business days instead of the 45 days allowed now, could not require a parent to appear in person, and could not charge for electronic copies.[^explanatory] The kinds of records parents can get would broaden to any student-specific files or materials the school keeps, and the current carve-outs for a teacher's personal notes and for health care records (which are handled by separate privacy laws) would be removed.[^explanatory] Parents would gain a stated right to prior notice when medical services are offered to their child, except in emergencies, and to notice of any medical service that could cost the parent money.[^explanatory] The requirement that schools immediately notify parents when a child is the alleged victim of abuse or assault by a school employee would be removed, as would the limit that notification of crimes applies only to events on school property during school hours.[^explanatory] The current statement that children have a right to a school setting free of discrimination on listed grounds would be replaced with a right to assurance that the school will not discriminate based on the family's sincerely held religious beliefs.[^explanatory] Parents' opt-out rights would expand to cover surveys or instruction about the child's sexual experiences or attractions, family beliefs or religion, political affiliations, and mental health.[^explanatory] The provisions on enrollment rights, language access, special education, harassment complaints, and the statement that the law creates no private right to sue would all be removed.[^explanatory] The school calendar, required fees, and dress code provisions would not change.[^explanatory]

If it fails, the current law, including the 2025 changes made by ESHB 1296, stays in effect.[^explanatory][^bill-1296] The initiative would take effect December 3, 2026, and would apply going forward.[^fiscal] KUOW reported that if voters approve the measure, the Legislature would have to wait two years before amending it.[^kuow-gov]

## Fiscal impact

A fiscal impact statement is a report the state Office of Financial Management must write for every initiative, describing any money the state or local governments would gain or lose.[^fiscal] OFM found that the initiative would have no fiscal impact on state government and would not change state or local revenue.[^fiscal] Local school districts might have minor costs to update their policies and procedures, but OFM says that cost cannot be estimated.[^fiscal]

## Arguments for

The official argument for the measure was written by Travis Reep, a public school elementary teacher; Alesha Perkins; Peter Rosenkranz, Superintendent, La Center School District; Tony Harris; Amy Sousa; and Jess Grant.[^arguments] They argue the measure restores parents' rights to their child's instructional materials and records, notice of medical services, opt-outs from certain activities, and immediate notification when law enforcement questions their child or their child leaves campus without permission.[^arguments] They say school staff remain mandatory reporters of suspected abuse, and that safeguards for at-risk children "should not become a default policy of withholding information from all parents."[^arguments] They argue current policies let schools withhold information when a child expresses a new gender identity at school, which they say undermines the parent-child relationship.[^arguments] In their rebuttal they say the measure "expressly states" that schools need not release mental health counseling records when a parent is the target of an abuse investigation, and that it "contains zero mandates placing students in danger."[^arguments]

## Arguments against

The official argument against the measure was written by John Lovick, State Senator, Democrat, former Snohomish County Sheriff; Roshelle Cleland, Washington Coalition Against Domestic Violence and Sexual Assault; Justin Gill, President, Washington State Nurses Association; Karen Crowley, President, League of Women Voters of Washington; Courtney Normand, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates; and Jaime Fineman-McKinney.[^arguments] They argue the measure would require schools to hand over sensitive information to parents who are under criminal investigation for harming their own child, and note there were 117,000 reports of child abuse or neglect in Washington in 2024.[^arguments] They say an unintended consequence of the measure is ending the current requirement that schools immediately notify parents when a child is the victim of a crime at school.[^arguments] They argue the measure would discourage students facing abuse or bullying from seeking help from counselors, and could put LGBTQ+ students at risk by "outing them to potentially abusive families."[^arguments] In their rebuttal they say the measure is "so poorly written it would eliminate key existing parental rights," including immediate notification when a child is a crime victim at school.[^arguments]

## Impacts you may not be thinking about

Both sides agree the measure changes the rules on notifying parents about crimes against their child, but they describe the change differently: the Attorney General's explanatory statement says the current specific right to immediate notice of abuse by a school employee would be removed, while supporters point to the initiative's general language about criminal actions.[^explanatory][^arguments] KUOW reported that Let's Go Washington says the measure does not require schools to notify a parent that a child is LGBTQ+ or transgender, but that it expands parental access to records when a child receives medical care, including mental health records.[^kuow-gov] The House Education Committee's staff analysis of the 2025 bill describes what that bill changed: it required school policies to prioritize student safety and privacy, revised the list of parental rights, added a statement of student rights, and added anti-retaliation protections for school employees.[^bill-1296-analysis] The initiative would reverse the parental-rights portion of those changes; the explanatory statement does not say it affects the student-rights or employee-protection parts of the 2025 law.[^explanatory] Several items the initiative would remove, such as enrollment rights for military families and homeless students, language access rights, and special education rights, are described by the Attorney General as rights that also exist elsewhere in state and federal law.[^explanatory]

## Related

The list of parental rights was created by Initiative 2081, which the Legislature passed in 2024 and which became law as Chapter 4, Laws of 2024.[^i2081][^explanatory] The 2025 changes this measure would undo were made by ESHB 1296, "Promoting a safe and supportive public education system," which became law as Chapter 369, Laws of 2025, effective May 20, 2025.[^bill-1296] The committee staff analysis of that bill summarizes what it changed.[^bill-1296-analysis] KUOW reported that the governor, who opposes the measure, said at a September 2026 press conference that it "actually weakens existing laws by removing parents' rights," and that the sponsor, Brian Heywood, said the measure "seeks to restore" parents' role as the primary stakeholders in raising their children.[^kuow-gov] The full text of the initiative and the official statements are published by the Secretary of State.[^fulltext][^explanatory][^fiscal][^arguments]
